Exam mode you can see from the front of the room.

A student’s calculator has an AI tutor and a camera. Here’s how to tell when they’re off, and a policy you can adapt.

At a glance

Look for the black bar.

In exam mode, the status bar across the top of the screen turns black and shows a padlock, the word EXAM and a timer that counts up from when exam mode started.

No black bar means it isn’t in exam mode. The calculator has no LED, so nothing glows or blinks either way: the screen is the only signal.

What it does

What exam mode turns off.

  • The AI, the camera and Wi-Fi

    All three switch off. The calculator keeps working as a scientific calculator.

  • Every app, notes included

    The apps behind MODE 4 are all off. Saved notes and past answers can’t be opened until exam mode ends, and turning it on closes any app that was open. MODE 4 just shows “Apps are off”.

  • What stays on

    The scientific calculator’s own features: fractions, trig, logs and memory, plus statistics and regression (MODE 2), function tables (MODE 3), the equation solver (MODE 5), complex numbers (MODE 6), base-n (MODE 7), matrices (MODE 8) and vectors (MODE 9), CALC, SOLVE, definite integrals and derivatives. Exam mode doesn’t switch these off, so if a test shouldn’t allow some of them, such as the equation solver or matrices, say so in your rules.

  • How a student turns it on

    SHIFT MODE 8 =, before the test starts.

  • How it ends

    Students can’t turn exam mode off from the keys. It ends after 12 hours or when a teacher unlocks it over USB.

Not allowed on the SAT, ACT or AP exams, even in exam mode, because it has Wi-Fi and a camera. Students should bring a standard calculator to those. It isn’t approved for any standardized test.

Classroom policy

Wording you can adapt.

Use it as is, change it, or set your own rule. It’s your classroom.

AI calculators are allowed for class work and homework.

For tests, exam mode must be on and visible on the screen before the test starts, or use a school calculator.

They aren’t allowed on the SAT, ACT or AP exams.

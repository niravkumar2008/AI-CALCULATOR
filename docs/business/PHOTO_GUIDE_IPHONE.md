# Product photos of the Proofline with an iPhone 15 Pro Max

Goal: photos as good as a store listing, for the website, the Kickstarter and ads, taken at home for about $40 of gear.
Everything shown must be real: real screens, real answers. The site's honesty rule applies to photos too.

## 1. Set up the phone once (5 minutes)
Settings → **Camera**:
- **Formats** → turn on **ProRAW & Resolution Control**, and set **Pro Default** to **ProRAW Max (up to 48 MP)**. RAW
  keeps the most detail and lets you fix colour later. Use HEIF Max 48 MP for quick shots.
- **Grid**: on. **Level**: on (shows when the phone is perfectly flat or straight).
- **Lens Correction**: on. **Macro Control**: on (shows a flower button so you decide when macro is used).
- **Preserve Settings**: turn on Creative Controls and ProRAW, so they don't reset.
- **Photographic Styles**: Standard (no warm or cool look baked in).

In the Camera app each time:
- Tap **RAW** (top right) so it's on.
- Turn off Live Photo and Night mode.
- Turn the flash off.

## 2. Gear (about $40)
| Item | Why | About |
|---|---|---|
| Phone tripod with a phone clamp | sharp photos, the same angle every shot | $20 |
| 2 white foam boards (20×30 in) | one curved as a white background, one to bounce light | $10 |
| Microfiber cloth + air blower | dust and fingerprints show at 48 MP | $8 |
| Cotton gloves (optional) | no fingerprints on the shell while you arrange it | $5 |

Use the Camera app's 3-second timer, or the Apple Watch camera remote, so pressing the shutter doesn't shake the phone.

## 3. Light: a window is your best light
- Shoot next to a large window with **indirect** daylight (no sun beam), late morning or afternoon.
- Put the calculator about 1–2 ft from the window, with the light coming from the **side** at about 45°.
- Hold the second white board on the other side, just out of the frame, to fill in the shadows.
- Turn off the room lights. Mixing yellow bulbs with daylight gives odd colours.
- Sun too harsh? Tape a white sheet or baking paper over the window to soften it.
- Shooting at night: two daylight (5600K) LED panels through a white sheet, one at 45° on each side. One stronger than
  the other gives a little shape.

## 4. Lens and distance: no fish-eye calculators
The wide 1× lens up close makes a rectangle look bent (bigger near the camera). Use the zoom lenses and step back:
- **2× (48 mm)** from about 1.5–2 ft: best all-round product shot.
- **5× (120 mm)** from about 4–5 ft: the flattest, most "studio" look, great for the straight-on hero. Use the tripod;
  at 5× every shake shows.
- **Macro** (the flower appears when you get close on 1×): key details such as the keys' texture, the camera window,
  the screen pixels. Get 2–4 in away.
- Fill about 70% of the frame and leave space around it for cropping (website, square, story sizes).

## 5. Focus and exposure (the screen is the hard part)
- Tap on the calculator to focus. **Long-press** to lock focus and exposure (AE/AF LOCK).
- Then **drag the sun icon down** a little (about −0.3 to −0.7). The colour screen is bright, and if it turns into a
  white blob it's overexposed.
- Turn the calculator's screen brightness to about 60–70%, not full.
- Show a **real** screen: a real AI Solve result for a real problem, or a calculation. No fake screens.
- Reflections: tilt the calculator 5–10° away from the window until the glare leaves the screen, or move the white
  board.

## 6. The shot list (what the website and ads need)
1. **Hero, straight-on, white background** (5×, tripod at screen height, calculator standing slightly tilted back):
   the main product image.
2. **3/4 angle** (2×, about 30° from the side, a little above): shows the shape and thickness.
3. **Low hero angle** (2×, camera low): makes it look premium. Good for the home page and ads.
4. **Side profile**: shows how thin it is.
5. **Back**: the camera window.
6. **Screen close-up** (macro or 2×): a real AI Solve with steps.
7. **In hand**: an adult hand holding it over a real homework problem, showing size and how it's used. College or adult
   setting (buyers are 18+).
8. **In use**: the calculator taking a photo of a problem on paper.
9. **Desk scene**: notebook, pencil, mug, soft daylight. Cover or turn away other brands' logos.
10. **What's in the box**: calculator, cable, quick-start card, box, laid flat from above (1×, phone flat, Level on).
11. **Video clips** (4K 30 fps, tripod): 5–10 s of pressing AI Solve and the answer appearing. Cinematic mode for a
    soft background.

For each, take 5–10 frames with tiny changes. You'll pick the best one later.

## 7. Editing (free)
- **Photos app** → Edit: Straighten/Crop first, then Exposure, Highlights (lower if the screen is bright), Shadows,
  Warmth (white board = pure white, not blue or yellow).
- **Clean Up** (Apple Intelligence, in Edit) removes dust specks and lint. Don't use it to change the product or the
  screen.
- For RAW files, Lightroom Mobile (free) has a white-balance picker: tap the white board.
- Keep a consistent look: same white level, same crop style for every product photo.

## Quick checklist on shoot day
- [ ] Calculator cleaned, no fingerprints or dust (check at 100% zoom)
- [ ] Room lights off, window light from the side, white board bounce
- [ ] Tripod, 2× or 5×, Level on, RAW on, timer on
- [ ] Focus/exposure locked and pulled down a little; the screen isn't blown out
- [ ] Real screen content
- [ ] All 11 shots from the list

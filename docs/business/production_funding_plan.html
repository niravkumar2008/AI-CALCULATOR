2. Three things that must happen before anyone pays

  1. Plain shells, not Casio ones.The 5 samples sit in genuine Casio fx-115ES shells. Selling a Casio shell with different insides is the classic "materially different goods" trademark case, even with the logo covered. Move the 4 boards into plain knockoff shells (about $6 each) before selling, and keep the Casio units as your own demo units.
  2. Sell them as paid "founder pre-orders"; ship after the FCC test.The ESP32 module's FCC approval covers only its radio. The whole calculator still needs its own Part 15B test (an SDoC). Before that, US rules (47 CFR 2.803) allow you to take payment if you tell the buyer in writing that delivery depends on the test and promise a full refund, but not to deliver. Budget about $700–1,500 for the test (offshore labs from about $660, US labs $3–5k). The same test covers every later batch if the design doesn't change, so it isn't wasted money.
  3. LLC + Indiana Registered Retail Merchant Certificate, and collect 7 % sales tax.You're "in business", so the casual-sale exemption doesn't apply. The certificate is $25 on INBiz. Get a product-liability insurance quote, and write a one-paragraph warranty and refund policy. If you're under 18, a parent is the account holder for Stripe, the bank and the LLC.
AlsoFilm the Kickstarter video and photos with all 5 samples before you send 4 away. Ship each one in a firm box with the device held still; one small installed cell (≈ 0.55 Wh) ships by USPS or UPS Ground with no special battery mark. Don't sell to minors without a parent buying, don't claim it's allowed in exams (it has Wi-Fi and a camera), and don't market it to children 12 and under.

3. "Slightly cheaper at more units" is really "a lot cheaper"

Worked out line by line from your cost model and supplier prices (knockoff shell, v15 LCD board, 150 mAh cell, about 35 % import duty):

  • The two big drops are 5 → 10 and 10 → 20. JLCPCB's fixed ≈ $77 per order and the shipping charges spread over more units, and at 20 the shells, LCDs, cameras and magnets switch from retail to Alibaba prices.
  • Build in 20s, not 25s. Shells have a minimum of 20 and magnets come in 20s, so 25 forces extras and saves only $1.46 a unit.
  • Duty doesn't shrink much: still about $9.50 a unit at 100, because the $800 duty-free limit is gone.
  • This line-by-line check comes out a little higher than the cost model ($113 vs $92 at 10, $47 vs $42 at 100), mainly because it charges duty per shipment. The plan uses the higher, safer numbers.

CHAPTER 1The China hub: one warehouse that collects everything

A freight forwarder or sourcing agent in Shenzhen gives you a warehouse address. Every supplier ships there by Chinese domestic post (1–3 days, almost free). The agent counts, photographs and repacks it all, then sends one box to you. This is called buyer's consolidation.

So is it faster? Here is the honest comparison

RouteFirst parts reach youWhole kit reaches youCustoms entries and courier feesQuality check before it leaves China
Each supplier ships to you by expressabout 5–10 daysabout 10–20 days (staggered)one per parcel (5–6 entries, each with its own fee)none: you find problems at home
Everything to a Shenzhen hub, then one express boxabout 10–18 daysabout 10–18 days (all together)one entry, one feeyes: counts and photos
Hub, then sea freight (LCL)about 35–50 days door to doorone entry; cheapest per kgyes

The hub doesn't make anything arrive sooner. Its value is that, since the US ended the $800 duty-free limit for every country (August 2025, written into CBP rules in June 2026), each separate parcel now goes through customs with its own duty and courier handling fee. Five parcels cost five sets of fees; one box costs one.

BatteriesLoose lithium cells ("UN3480") can only fly on cargo planes, at no more than 30 % charge, with dangerous-goods paperwork, and many forwarders refuse them. Two easy ways round it: for batch 1, buy the 100 cells from a US seller (Adafruit #1317 at $5.95, about $300 more than Alibaba but no shipping trouble); from batch 3, let the factory install the cell, because a battery inside the product ("UN3481, contained in equipment") ships far more easily. Since 1 Jan 2026 even cells packed beside a device must be at ≤ 30 % charge for air.

Duty: what you'll pay at the border

Tariffs on Chinese goods changed four times in 2025–26. The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026; what's left for most Chinese electronics is the Section 301 tariff (7.5–25 % depending on the list) plus a 12.5 % layer added in July 2026, so plan on about 20–40 %. Your cost model uses 35 %, which sits inside that range. Sources disagree on the exact layer, so before batch 1:

  1. Search CBP's rulings database (CROSS) for "calculator parts".An existing ruling on a similar part tells you the code and rate for free, in minutes.
  2. If nothing fits, ask CBP for a binding ruling through eRulings.Free; usually 30–90 days. Do it right after the Kickstarter launches so the answer is back before you order.
  3. For batch 1, let the forwarder quote "DDP" (delivered duty paid).They pay the duty and add it to the invoice, so nothing surprises you at the door. Ask for an itemised quote that shows the duty line.

Setting up the hub (do it before batch 1, not now)

  1. Pick 2–3 forwarders and ask the same questions.Do you accept parcels from several Alibaba sellers? Fee per inbound parcel and for repacking? Photos on arrival? Express and air-freight DDP price to Indiana for 15 kg? Do you handle lithium batteries (licence, UN38.3 papers)?
  2. Send each Alibaba seller the hub's address and your order number for the label.Pay with Trade Assurance, never more than 30 % deposit; pay the rest after the hub's photos show the right parts and counts.
  3. Test it once with a small order.The knockoff shell samples or the bulk samples are a good dry run: a few parcels, low risk.

Other money worth chasing (no repayment)

Free money

  • Student and youth pitch competitions and school or state entrepreneurship grants. Prizes of $500–5,000 are common and a working demo wins them.
  • Local SBDC (Small Business Development Center): free advice, and they know local grants and microlenders.
  • SCORE mentors and university IP clinics: free help with the trade-dress question.

Money that costs something

  • Kiva: 0 %, up to $15,000; you first raise from your own network for 15 days, then the public lends.
  • SBA microloan: up to $50,000 through a local intermediary; slower, may need collateral.
  • Shopify Capital / revenue-based financing: only after sales history; repaid as a share of sales.
  • Avoid: credit cards and selling a share of the company this early.

CHAPTER 3How Kickstarter works, step by step

Kickstarter is all-or-nothing pre-orders. You set a goal and a deadline. People pledge. If the total reaches the goal by the deadline, their cards are charged and you get the money; if not, nobody is charged and you owe nothing. You're promising to make and ship a product, so the work is in getting the right people ready before day one.

Before launch (the part that decides success)

  1. Check who can be the creator.Kickstarter requires the account holder to be 18+ with a US bank account and ID. If you're under 18, a parent or guardian creates the project with you and enters their identity and bank details; they're also responsible for delivery. Use your Indiana LLC's bank account once it exists.
  2. Build the waitlist.Your marketing plan's core number. About 2–10 % of engaged sign-ups back on launch day, so 40 backers needs roughly 400–2,000 emails. Post the real samples 3–5 times a week (short video), and collect emails on your site.
  3. Turn on a Kickstarter pre-launch page.People click "notify me", and Kickstarter emails them the moment you go live. Point your videos at it for the last 4–6 weeks.
  4. Make the page and the video.Your draft is in docs/business/KICKSTARTER_DRAFT.md: honest "a tutor, not a shortcut" pitch, only real footage of the samples, what's done vs not done yet, risks and a delivery estimate you can keep.
  5. Set the rewards.From your draft: $5 thanks · $195 Batch 1 (limited to the first batch) · $210 calculator · $285 Batch 1 + a year of the plan · $299 calculator + a year. US shipping only, charged at pledge at cost + 10 % (Kickstarter's fee applies to shipping too).
  6. Set the goal from costs, not hopes.$9,500 covers 100 units plus the one-time costs after fees (raise it to $13–16k if you need your own shell). A goal you can hit in the first 48 hours makes the project look strong; it can raise more than the goal.
  7. Submit for review.Kickstarter checks hardware projects for a working prototype and honest claims (no photorealistic renders presented as the product). Allow a few days; launch on a Tuesday morning.

During the 30 days

  1. Day 1: email the whole waitlist, post everywhere.Most campaigns get a third of their total in the first two days; that's what puts you on Kickstarter's own pages.
  2. Post an update every few days.New footage, a test result, answers to common questions. Reply to every comment.
  3. Watch the middle slump; plan the last 48 hours.Pledges dip in the middle week and jump at the end when the "ending soon" reminders go out.

After it ends

  1. Cards are charged; failed ones get 7 days to fix.If a few fail, you still get what was collected, even if it ends up slightly under the goal.
  2. Money arrives about 14 days after the end(plus 3–14 business days for your bank). Fees come out first: 5 % to Kickstarter and about 3 % + $0.20 per pledge for card processing, so roughly 8–10 % in total.
  3. Send the pledge survey.Collect shipping addresses (US only) and any add-ons, through Kickstarter's survey or a pledge manager. Keep "late pledges" on so people can still order.
  4. Order the parts the day the money lands(chapter 4 has the calendar). Hold back the AI-plan money from the plan bundles: about $65 per bundle stays in the bank until each year of service is used.
  5. Build, test, ship, and update every 2 weeks until every reward is delivered.Silence is what makes backers angry, not delays. If something slips, say so early with the new date.

CHAPTER 5Rules that keep the money safe, and the risks to watch

Rules

  • Never order a batch before its money is in the bank.
  • Keep 10 % of every batch budget as contingency.
  • Alibaba: Trade Assurance, chat on-platform, ≤ 30 % deposit, balance only after the hub's photos.
  • Keep the AI-plan money (≈ $65 per plan bundle) aside until that year of service is used.
  • Business money in the LLC's bank account, never mixed with personal money.
  • Every number that's a guess gets replaced with a real quote before you set the Kickstarter goal.

Risks

  • Trade dress: the knockoff shell may not be safe to sell publicly; get the lawyer's view before the goal is set.
  • Tariffs: changed four times in 18 months; a 10-point jump adds ≈ $3–4 a unit at 100.
  • Batteries: forwarders refuse loose cells; buy them in the US for batch 1.
  • Too few backers: the goal is all-or-nothing, so a missed goal costs time, not money.
  • Your time: 100 units ≈ 60 hours of assembly; past ~300 a factory has to take over.
  • FCC: no unit (not even the 4 prototypes) may be delivered before the Part 15B test passes; pre-orders with a written disclosure and refund promise are fine.
  • Age: Kickstarter, Kiva and banks need an 18+ account holder; if that isn't you yet, a parent runs those accounts.

Decisions only you can make

  1. Knockoff shell or your own shell for public sales?Book the law-clinic consult; the answer sets the goal ($9,500 vs $13–16k).
  2. Who is the Kickstarter creator and the bank account holder?You (18+) or a parent with you, through the LLC.
  3. Pilot size: 4 or 5 units?Fits the $2,000 (chapter 2, rung 0).
  4. Launch month.Spring 2027 per the marketing plan; pick the month only when the waitlist passes ~400.

## 0. The short version

1. **Students already use AI for homework, a lot.** Between May and December 2025, AI use for
   homework went from 48% to 62% of US middle, high school and college students; for high
   schoolers it went from 49% to 63% [S18]. Two thirds of students (67%) also said AI use
   harms critical thinking [S18]. That second number is our opening: **"shows every step so you learn"**.
2. **The money is shifting from "answer sites" to AI.** Chegg lost 31% of its subscribers in a
   year (3.2 million left in Q1 2025) and cut 22% of staff, blaming AI tools and Google's AI
   answers [S21]. Students will pay, but they now expect AI-speed help.
3. **Our closest competitor, VovoCorp, is priced almost exactly where we are, and gives more
   for free.** Its camera model is **$239.90 (pre-order)**, and it includes **82 AI uses a day
   "free and forever"**, with Premium at about $7/month or $59.90/year [S1]. We are $225 with
   one free month, then $10/month or $100/year and **no free tier** (PAGE_BRIEF). This is our
   biggest commercial risk.
4. **VovoCorp sells stealth.** It acts as "a classic 82ms calculator" until a secret PIN is
   entered [S1], an "emergency button" snaps back to the normal screen [S4], and its TikTok
   promises "100% accuracy" [S2]. Press describe these devices with "hidden password" and
   "concealed camera" [S30b]. **We are the opposite: visible exam mode, honest limits.** That is
   the clearest gap in the market.
5. **No AI calculator is allowed on the big tests.** The SAT, AP and ACT all ban calculators
   with Wi-Fi, cameras or internet [S23][S24][S25]. We must say this loudly; it builds trust.
6. **Phones are being banned in school.** 20 states had full "bell-to-bell" bans of wireless
   devices in 2025-26, and 41 states have passed some law about phones in school [S29]. This
   cuts both ways: "studying without your phone" is attractive, but **some of these laws ban any
   wireless device, which may include ours** (my reading; needs a check per state).
7. **Teachers worry mainly about cheating.** 74% of teachers who described an AI dilemma said it
   was about cheating [S27]. Only 18% of teachers said their school had a specific generative AI
   policy [S27b]. Teachers are a risk (bans) and a channel (NumWorks won France through
   teachers [S16]).
8. **Free phone apps are the real "default" competitor.** Photomath, Mathway, Gauth and Brainly
   all have free tiers and charge about $10/month for the full thing [S6][S7][S8][S9]. Gauth
   (ByteDance) was a top-ranked US education app in 2025 [S12]. Our device must justify itself
   against "free on your phone".
9. **AI gadgets fail when they over-promise.** Humane's $699 AI Pin plus $24/month died within a
   year; Rabbit's R1 sold 100,000 preorders and then got brutal reviews [S31]. Honest demos of a
   real, working device are the cure.
10. **Back-to-school is the season.** US K-12 households planned to spend $293 on electronics
    and college shoppers $342 in 2026, and 62% had started shopping by early July [S22]. The
    2027 back-to-school window (June to September 2027) is the natural first sales season.

### 2.2 What to learn from each

- **VovoCorp:** proves people will pay about $240 for an AI calculator with a camera, and that
  TikTok works for it. Its weak spots are stealth marketing (a reputational and platform risk),
  self-hosted reviews only, "100% accuracy" claims (an FTC-type risk, see section 8), and no US
  focus (Spanish-first, fx-82MS layout). **Never copy their tone. Never attack them by name in
  public either**; let the contrast speak.
- **Photomath / Mathway / Gauth:** their steps-behind-a-paywall model annoys students [S8b].
  Our plan includes every step on every solve. That is a real, honest difference.
- **Gauth's cancellation complaints** [S8b] show why our "cancel anytime from your account page,
  no card for the free month" is worth saying out loud.
- **NumWorks:** the model for winning teachers. They put teachers at the centre of product and
  go-to-market (engineers spent more than half their time with teachers) and became the leader
  in France's graphing-calculator market within five years [S16]. They give free samples,
  workshops and a free emulator [S17]. **Our `/try-it/` browser simulator is our version of the
  emulator.**
- **Casio / TI:** they own "allowed in exams". We will never be, so we do not compete there. We
  are the homework and study device, and we tell students to keep a $23 standard calculator for
  test day.

   (hide it)                       |                         (teacher can see)

          (secret PIN, panic       |
           button)                 |

**Our spot (top right) is empty.** No one else sells an AI calculator built to be seen by the
teacher. Own it.

**Positioning statement (internal):** For high-school and college STEM students who want help
without their phone, Proofline is a real scientific calculator with an AI tutor that shows every
step, and an exam mode a teacher can see from the front of the room. Unlike "ChatGPT
calculators" that hide their AI, Proofline is honest by design.

### 6.2 What the numbers say

- **Our subscription price is in line with the market.** $10/month matches Photomath, Mathway
  and Brainly ($9.99) and is below Gauth ($11.99). The yearly $100 is above Mathway and Brainly
  ($39.99/yr) and Photomath ($69.99/yr) [S6][S7][S9]. The earlier $15/month idea would have
  been out of line; $10 is right.
- **The device price is in line with VovoCorp's camera model** ($225 vs $239.90) [S1].
- **The weak spot is the free tier.** VovoCorp gives 82 AI uses a day free forever [S1]; all
  phone apps have free tiers. We go to zero AI after month one. Honest messaging helps
  ("without the plan it's still a full offline calculator"), but expect this objection in
  every comment section.
- **Options for Nirav to decide (not for marketing to announce):**
  1. Keep as is and lean on "every step, no ads, no phone" (simplest, cheapest to run).
  2. Add a small free allowance after month one (for example a few solves a week) so the AI
     never "switches off" completely. Costs money per user; check the cost model first.
  3. Make yearly the default and show "$8.33 a month" honestly (already in PAGE_BRIEF).
  4. A preorder-only perk that is real and costs little, such as the first batch's serial
     numbers or an extra free month for waitlist members. Only if it is actually honoured.

I recommend 1 + 3 now, and decide on 2 after measuring real solve costs on the 5 samples.
Any perk must be real, written down, and never presented with fake urgency.

## 7. Lessons from other student and hardware launches

| Launch | What happened | Lesson for Proofline | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NumWorks** (2017) | Built with math teachers; teachers at the centre of go-to-market; leader in France within 5 years; free samples, PD workshops and emulator | Teachers are a channel. The `/teachers/` page plus free sample loans later. The simulator is our emulator | [S16][S17] |
| **Playdate** (Panic) | Revealed 2019, teased slowly for two years; preorders opened July 2021 with a week's email notice; 20,000 sold in under 20 minutes; "won't sell out", ordering early just means shipping sooner; delays to 2022 and beyond; 70,000+ shipped | Slow honest reveals work. A "batch" model ("order earlier, ship sooner") is urgency without lying. Expect delays and say so early | [S32] |
| **reMarkable** (2016) | Preorders at $379 opened ~9 months before shipping to build publicity; first 11,000 shipped Aug 2017 | Long preorder windows can work but need constant honest updates | [S33] |
| **Flipper Zero** (2020) | Kickstarter goal $60,000, raised $4.88 million from 37,987 backers, $1M on day one; later went viral on TikTok | A big pre-launch community (Discord/email) makes day one. TikTok virality can also bring a "hacking/cheating" image you don't control | [S34] |
| **Humane AI Pin / Rabbit R1** (2024) | Hype, then poor reviews; Humane ($699 + $24/mo) shut down Feb 2025; Rabbit sold 100,000 R1 preorders then got "barely reviewable" verdicts | Never promise features that don't work yet. Show the real device doing real things. A monthly fee on top of a device must be clearly worth it | [S31] |
| **Robinhood** waitlist | Referral "move up the list" waitlist reached about 1 million sign-ups before launch | A referral waitlist is cheap growth. Keep rewards real (earlier batch, not fake prizes) | [S38] |

## 8. Rules that shape the marketing (summary; checklist in MARKETING_GUIDE.md)

- **Exams:** SAT bans devices with wireless internet, Bluetooth, cellular, or built-in cameras
  [S23]. AP bans models with wireless capability, internet access, cameras or "smartphone-type
  features" [S24]. ACT bans cameras and wireless connectivity (secondary source; check act.org)
  [S25]. The engineering FE exam allows only certain Casio fx-115/fx-991, HP and TI models
  [S26].
- **Influencers:** FTC Endorsement Guides (updated June 2023): disclosure must be clear and in
  the video itself, not just in the caption or profile; free products count as a material
  connection; "#ad", "ad", "sponsored" are fine, "sp", "collab" are not [S46].
- **Reviews:** FTC rule on fake reviews in force since Oct 21, 2024: no fake, bought, or
  insider reviews, no buying followers or views; fines up to about $52,000 per violation
  [S47].
- **AI claims:** FTC "Operation AI Comply" (from Sept 2024, continuing in 2025) targets
  overstated AI claims [S48]. Don't say "100% accurate", "always right", or "replaces a tutor".
- **Teens and privacy:** COPPA covers under-13s; the amended rule took effect June 23, 2025 with
  compliance by April 22, 2026 [S49]. New York's Child Data Protection Act (since June 20, 2025)
  limits collecting data from under-18 New Yorkers without need or consent [S50]. CCPA likely
  does not apply to us yet (thresholds such as $26.625M revenue or 100,000 California
  consumers), but its under-16 "no selling data without opt-in" rule is a good habit [S51].
- **Ads to under-18s:** TikTok allows only age, language, country/city and device targeting for
  under-18s in North America; no interests, gender or custom audiences, and no lead-generation
  objective [S43]. Meta limits teen ad targeting to age and location (since Feb 2023) [S44].
- **Creators:** TikTok requires creators to be 18+ to use branded-content tools; Meta's creator
  marketplace is 18+ [S45]. **Pay only 18+ creators.**
- **Subscriptions:** The FTC's "click-to-cancel" rule was struck down on July 8, 2025, and the FTC
  restarted rulemaking in March 2026; ROSCA and state laws still apply [S52]. California's
  amended auto-renewal law (from July 1, 2025) covers free-to-paid conversions, requires
  express consent, annual reminders and online cancellation [S53]. Our free month needs no card
  and does not auto-convert, which keeps us on the safe side; the paid plans must follow these
  rules.
- **Preorders:** FTC Mail Order Rule: you must have a reasonable basis for the shipping time you
  state (or 30 days if you state none), and must get consent or refund if delayed [S54].
- **Email:** CAN-SPAM: real postal address (a PO box is fine), working unsubscribe honoured
  within 10 business days [S55].

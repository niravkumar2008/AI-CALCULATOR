# Proofline marketing guide

For Nirav. Written 2026-10-11. This is the playbook: what to say, where, how often, what it
costs, and what never to do. It assumes no marketing background.

- Facts and numbers come from `MARKET_RESEARCH.md`. Tags like **[S20]** point to its source list
  (section 10 there), with links and dates.
- The day-by-day plan for the first 12 weeks is in `CONTENT_CALENDAR.md`.
- Product facts: only what is in `web/site-src/PAGE_BRIEF.md`. If it's not there, don't say it.

## 0. The strategy in five lines

1. **Own the honest spot.** Every other AI calculator sells secrecy (secret PINs, "emergency"
   buttons). We sell the opposite: *shows every step so you learn* and *exam mode a teacher can
   see from the front of the room*. Make that the brand.
2. **Show, don't claim.** Short videos of the real device doing real things (and saying "I can't
   read that" when it can't) on TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, 3 to 5 posts a week.
   The build log of the 5 samples is the story until then.
3. **Own the list.** Every post points to one thing: the waitlist. Email is the only channel no
   algorithm can take away. Aim for thousands, because only 2% to 10% of a list typically buys at
   launch [S37].
4. **Adults pay, adults promote.** Creators, ads and the buyer are 18+. Teens (13+) are reached
   only through organic content and honest messages; no teen ad targeting, no paid teen creators.
5. **Preorders when the device is proven, timed for back-to-school 2027.** A short
   all-or-nothing crowdfunding campaign (or own-site reservations) in spring 2027, after the
   samples are tested, with no shipping date promised until production is real.

### 1.4 Brand voice

Short, plain, confident, honest. Like a smart friend who is good at math and doesn't brag.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "AI can make mistakes. That's why it shows every step." | "100% accurate", "never wrong" |
| "Not allowed on the SAT, ACT or AP. Bring a standard calculator." | "Exam-approved", "teacher-proof", "they'll never know" |
| "Preorders are planned for 2027." | "Shipping soon!", "Only 50 left!" |
| "$225, plus the AI tutor plan: $10 a month or $100 a year. First month free, no card." | Hiding the plan, "starting at $0" |
| "Prototype photos coming soon." | Renders passed off as photos |
| Sentence case, no exclamation marks | "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "unlock", "seamless", "hack" |

**Words that are banned in all content, captions, comments and DMs:** cheat, cheating (except
"this is not for cheating"), undetectable, stealth, hidden mode, secret mode, panic button, hide
button, exam-approved, pass any test, teacher won't know. Also never name the AI vendor, the
payment vendor, or Casio (except the footer's "Not affiliated with Casio Computer Co., Ltd.").

**The Casio logo problem:** the prototype samples use genuine fx-115ES shells, which carry
Casio's name. **Never show the Casio logo in any video or photo.** Cover it (a neat Proofline
sticker over the logo area is fine), crop it out, or film from angles where it isn't visible.
Ask a lawyer about the shell's look before preorders (trade dress).

## 2. Channel plan

Teens' top platforms: YouTube 90%, TikTok 63%, Instagram 61%, Snapchat 55%, Reddit 14% [S20].
Most of our audience is on the first three. **Make one vertical video, post it to all three.**

### 2.1 TikTok (main channel)

- **Why:** it's where students discover gadgets, and where VovoCorp sells [S2]. Short videos of a
  physical device doing something surprising work well here.
- **What to post:** demos (AI Solve, exam mode, apps), build log, honest Q&A replies to comments,
  "things it won't do".
- **Format:** 9:16 vertical, 15 to 45 seconds. Hook in the first 1 to 2 seconds. On-screen text
  (many watch muted). Hands and the device, no faces needed. Real key sounds are a feature.
- **Hooks that fit our voice:**
  - "My calculator just told me it can't read my handwriting."
  - "This is what my calculator looks like when a teacher turns on exam mode."
  - "I built a calculator that shows its work. Sample 1 of 5."
  - "POV: you're stuck on homework at 11 pm and your phone is in another room."
  - "Things my AI calculator will NOT do."
- **Cadence:** 3 to 5 posts a week. Buffer's study of 11.4 million posts found 2 to 5 posts a week
  gives the biggest jump per post (+17% views vs once a week) and more posts mainly add more
  "lottery tickets" for a hit [S39]. Quality and consistency beat volume.
- **Engagement:** reply to comments within a day; turn good questions into reply videos (TikTok's
  "reply with video"). Pin a comment with honest facts (price, not for the SAT, waitlist).
- **Link:** the waitlist link in bio. Use a link with a source tag (see section 8).
- **Account:** a business account under "Proofline". Use only commercially licensed sounds (the
  platform's commercial sound library), not trending songs.

### 2.2 YouTube Shorts and YouTube

- **Why:** 90% of teens use YouTube [S20]; Shorts can be up to 3 minutes [S57]; videos are
  searchable for years.
- **What:** the same verticals as TikTok (remove the TikTok watermark, export the clean file).
  Plus, once a month, one longer video (5 to 10 minutes): "Building Proofline, part 1",
  "Exam mode explained for teachers", "Every app on the calculator".
- **Titles:** plain and searchable: "AI calculator that shows every step (exam mode demo)".
- **Cadence:** same days as TikTok; one long video monthly from week 5.

### 2.3 Instagram Reels

- **Why:** 61% of teens use Instagram [S20]; college students and teachers are there; Reels are
  shareable to Stories and DMs.
- **What:** the same vertical videos, plus a few carousels: "How exam mode works in 4 pictures",
  "First year cost, honestly", "What's in the Apps menu".
- **Cadence:** same videos as TikTok; 1 carousel a week from week 3.

### 2.4 Reddit

- **Why:** where college STEM students and gadget fans ask for real opinions. Small reach, high
  trust, and posts rank in Google.
- **Rules:** subreddit rules first. Many communities ban self-promotion or allow it only in
  certain threads; the common rule is that 90% of your activity should be genuinely helpful and
  only about 10% about your product; always disclose that you're the maker; never use extra
  accounts; never DM people unasked [S56].
- **What:** honest build-log posts in maker and hardware communities ("I designed a calculator PCB
  with a camera; here's what went wrong"), only where the rules allow. Answer questions in study
  communities without linking unless asked.
- **Cadence:** 1 helpful comment a day is plenty; 1 build-log post a month where allowed.
- **Never** post in exam-cheating threads, even to say "ours isn't for cheating".

### 2.5 Discord (community)

- **Why:** a home for early fans and future preorder customers; Flipper Zero built a community of
  more than 100,000 [S34].
- **When:** open it once the waitlist passes about 300 people (an empty server looks dead).
- **Rules:** 13+ (Discord's own minimum); a pinned code of conduct; no exam-cheating talk
  (instant removal); you never DM members first.
- **Channels:** #announcements, #build-log, #feature-requests, #study-help (members helping each
  other), #show-your-desk.
- **Rituals:** a weekly 10-minute "build update" post; a monthly live Q&A (voice); early
  members name a sample, vote on a colour of the box, beta-test the `/try-it/` simulator.

### 2.6 Creators and UGC

See section 4 for the full program. Short version: 18+ only, STEM and study creators, college
students, teachers and tutors, paid a flat fee, loaned a sample, disclosure in the video.

### 2.7 Teachers and schools

- **Why:** NumWorks became the leader in France by putting teachers at the centre [S16]; 74% of
  teachers' AI worries are about cheating [S27]. If teachers trust us, they stop being the
  obstacle.
- **What we already have:** `/teachers/` ("Exam mode you can see from the front of the room.")
  and `/honest/` ("A tutor, not a shortcut.").
- **What to do (no cold outreach until there is a working sample):**
  - A one-page PDF for teachers (exam mode, USB unlock, AI/camera/Wi-Fi off, not for SAT/ACT/AP,
    no photos stored).
  - A 60-second "Exam mode explained for teachers" video.
  - From spring 2027: **a free loan of a sample to a few teachers (adults) for honest feedback,**
    with written permission to quote them only if they want to be quoted, and disclosure that
    the loan was free.
  - Teacher creators (18+, of course) are excellent partners; they get paid like any creator.
- **Never:** sell to schools before the product ships, claim schools "approve" it, or contact
  students through teachers.

### 2.8 SEO and blog

- **Why:** people search "AI calculator", "calculator that shows steps", "is an AI calculator
  allowed on the SAT". Answering honestly earns trust and links.
- **First 6 articles (one every two weeks, 600 to 1,000 words):**
  1. "Are AI calculators allowed on the SAT, ACT and AP? (No. Here's what to bring.)" (cite the
     official policies [S23][S24][S25])
  2. "How Proofline's exam mode works"
  3. "Answer vs steps: why seeing the working matters"
  4. "Building a calculator from scratch: sample 1 of 5"
  5. "What the AI tutor plan costs in the first year" ($325 yearly, $335 monthly)
  6. "Studying without your phone: a setup that works"
- **Basics:** one topic per page, plain title, the FAQ format already on the site, a link to the
  waitlist at the end. No keyword stuffing.

### 2.9 Email (the most important channel)

- **The list is the business.** Social posts drive sign-ups; email turns sign-ups into
  preorders.
- **Welcome email (immediately):** what Proofline is, honest status, the `/try-it/` simulator
  link, "reply and tell us what class you'd use it for" (replies are gold research).
- **Monthly update (first Tuesday of each month):** build progress with real photos, one thing
  that went wrong, what's next, one question.
- **Before preorders:** a 4-email sequence (section 9).
- **Legal basics:** a real postal address in every email (a PO box is fine) and a working
  unsubscribe honoured within 10 business days [S55]. Only email people who signed up.

## 4. Creator and UGC program

### 4.1 Rules (non-negotiable)

- **Creators must be 18 or older.** TikTok's branded-content tools require 18+; Meta's creator
  marketplace is 18+ [S45]. No exceptions, even if a 17-year-old asks.
- **Disclosure in the video itself** (spoken and on-screen in the first seconds: "Ad" or
  "Proofline loaned me this and paid me for this video"), plus the platform's paid-partnership
  label. Captions or bio alone are not enough [S46].
- **Honest opinions only.** They can say what they didn't like. We never pay for "positive"
  content and never script fake reactions [S47].
- **A content brief, not a script:** the must-say facts (price incl. plan, not for SAT/ACT/AP,
  AI can make mistakes) and the banned words list (section 1.4). We review for facts and
  compliance, not for tone.
- **No cheating jokes, no "hide it from the teacher" bits**, no filming in real exams or
  classrooms, no minors on camera.
- **A short written agreement** (one page): fee, deliverables, usage rights, disclosure, return of
  the loaned sample, and our right to ask for corrections of factual errors.

### 4.2 Who to work with

| Type | Why | Size |
|---|---|---|
| College STEM students who post study content (18+) | Same audience as Segment A; authentic | 5k to 50k followers |
| Math and science teachers and tutors who post (adults) | Trust; can talk about exam mode credibly | 5k to 100k |
| Gadget and maker reviewers | Segment D, preorder buyers, press pickup | 10k to 100k |
| UGC creators (make videos for our account, don't post themselves) | Cheap, fast content variety | n/a |

Look for: high comments-to-views ratio, comments that are real questions, an audience that skews
18+ (ask for audience age stats; a creator whose audience is mostly under 18 is a poor fit for
paid content).

### 4.3 What it costs (2025-26 market rates)

| Item | Typical price | Source |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok micro creator (10k to 50k followers), one video | $150 to $700; Spark Ads permission adds $50 to $300 | [S40] |
| Instagram micro creator, Reel | $250 to $2,500 (feed post $150 to $1,500) | [S40] |
| YouTube micro creator (10k to 50k), one video | $500 to $5,000 | [S40] |
| UGC video (no posting), entry level | $50 to $100; mid-level $150 to $500; usage rights can double or triple the price | [S41] |

**Our offer (my recommendation):** a flat **$150 to $300** per TikTok/Reel creator video, a loaned
sample (insured, returned), and a small extra ($50 to $100) for 30-day permission to boost it as
an ad. Start with 2 creators, measure waitlist sign-ups from each one's tagged link, then do
more of what works.

### 4.4 The loaner program (we only have 5 samples)

- Keep 2 samples at home (filming, testing). Rotate 3 as loaners.
- Prepaid return label and a simple checklist in the box. Ship insured.
- Loan length: 10 days. One loan at a time per creator.
- Track: who has which serial, ship date, return date.

### 4.5 Affiliate and referral (later, after preorders open)

- An affiliate code for creators (for example, a flat fee per preorder) only once there is
  something to buy. Disclose it like any other paid relationship.
- Never offer rewards for reviews of a particular sentiment [S47].

## 7. Metrics and simple tracking

### 7.1 What to measure (weekly)

| Metric | Where | Why | Target (my estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Waitlist sign-ups (new this week, total)** | Waitlist database | The one number that matters | Weeks 1 to 6: 20 to 50/week; after demos: 100+/week |
| Sign-ups by source | Waitlist `source` field (see 7.2) | Which channel works | n/a |
| Views per post, average watch time, shares | TikTok/YouTube/IG analytics | Which hooks work | Average watch time above 50% of length |
| Profile visits and link clicks | Platform analytics | Is the CTA working | Link click rate above 1% of views |
| Comments asking "where can I get one" | Manual | Purchase intent | Growing week by week |
| Email open rate and replies | Email tool | List health | Opens above 40%, replies every send |
| Cost per sign-up (paid only) | Ad spend / sign-ups from that source | Is paid worth it | Under $2 per sign-up |
| Discord members and weekly active | Discord | Community health | n/a |

### 7.2 Tracking setup (simple)

- A spreadsheet with one row per post: date, platform, idea number, hook, views, likes, shares,
  comments, link clicks, notes.
- **A tag per channel in the waitlist link,** for example `proofline.example/?src=tt` (TikTok),
  `?src=yt`, `?src=ig`, `?src=rd` (Reddit), `?src=c-alex` (creator Alex).
- **Engineering note (done, Oct 2026, not deployed yet):** every waitlist form now sends the link's
  `?utm_source=`, `?utm_medium=`, `?utm_campaign=` (the short `?src=tt` counts as `utm_source`)
  and `?ref=` (referral code), kept for the whole visit; the daily cap is now 20,000 new sign-ups
  (an abuse backstop only); and every form has a required "I'm 13 or older" checkbox that the API
  also enforces. Counts by channel: `docs/web/GUIDE.md` section 8.1 (admin-only D1 queries). Tags
  must be lower-case letters, digits, `-` or `_`, up to 40 characters (`c-alex`, not `C. Alex`).
  Still open: Turnstile (required before launch) and double opt-in (a confirm-your-email link) to
  keep the list clean.

### 7.3 The conversion maths (why the list must be big)

Pre-launch lists typically turn 2% to 5% of sign-ups into backers, and well-nurtured, engaged
lists 5% to 15% or more [S37]. So:

| Waitlist size | Preorders at 3% | At 10% |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 30 | 100 |
| 5,000 | 150 | 500 |
| 10,000 | 300 | 1,000 |

Work backwards from the first production batch size to set the waitlist goal.

## 8. Growing the waitlist

1. **One clear call to action** everywhere: "Join the waitlist" (`/` and the footer form).
2. **Give a reason to join now that is true:** "Waitlist members hear first and get the first
   preorder batch" (Playdate's "order earlier, ship sooner" model [S32]). No fake scarcity.
3. **Referral "move up the list"** (Robinhood reached about 1 million sign-ups this way [S38]):
   later, give each member a personal link; every friend who joins moves them up a batch. Real
   reward only (earlier batch), no prizes that need a sweepstakes lawyer.
4. **The `/try-it/` simulator** as the hook: "Try it free in your browser, then join the list."
5. **Pin a waitlist post** on each platform; end cards on videos.
6. **Reply to every "where can I buy?" comment** with the honest status and the link.
7. **Monthly email** so the list stays warm (a cold list converts badly).
8. **Ask one question in the welcome email** ("Which class would you use it for?"). Use the
   answers to choose the next video.

## 9. Turning the waitlist into preorders

1. **Survey the list 6 to 8 weeks before preorders:** "Would you preorder at $225 + the plan?
   Yearly or monthly? Which month?" This sets the funding goal and batch size.
2. **The 4-email sequence:**
   - **Email 1 (4 weeks before):** "Preorders open on [date]. Here's what's ready, what isn't, and
     what it costs." Full honest price.
   - **Email 2 (2 weeks before):** demo video plus the honest FAQ (not for SAT/ACT/AP, photos,
     AI mistakes, what happens without the plan).
   - **Email 3 (2 days before):** "Doors open Thursday at 10 am Eastern. Batch 1 goes to the
     earliest orders." (True if you fill orders in order.)
   - **Email 4 (launch day):** the link, nothing else.
3. **Launch on a weekday morning** with creators and your own posts going live the same hour.
4. **Daily honest updates** during the campaign (progress, questions answered).
5. **After launch:** a monthly production update to every buyer, including bad news first.

## 10. Preorder platform: recommendation

| Option | Fees | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kickstarter** | 5% platform + about 3% + $0.20 per pledge in the US [S35] | All-or-nothing: if the goal isn't met, no one is charged and you owe nothing; gadget fans browse it; validation for press; Design & Technology had its biggest year in 2025 [S36] | About 8% to 10% total; only about 23% of Technology projects succeed overall [S36]; creator must be 18+; you can't sell the subscription there |
| **Indiegogo** (owned by Gamefound since July 2025) | 5% + 3% + $0.20 [S35] | Similar to Kickstarter | Fixed (all-or-nothing) funding only since Oct 16, 2025; payment change caused friction; smaller audience [S35] |
| **Own site** (store already built; `STORE_OPEN` switch) | Payment processing only | Keep the customer relationship; the plan and account linking are integrated; no platform fee | No discovery; all risk on you; preorders are sales: the FTC Mail Order Rule requires a reasonable basis for the stated ship time, and consent or a refund when delayed [S54]; long gaps between payment and delivery raise chargebacks [S59] |
| **Own site "reserve, pay at ship"** | None until shipping | No legal or refund risk; honest | Weak commitment; doesn't fund production |

**Recommendation:**

1. **First batch: Kickstarter, all-or-nothing, in spring 2027,** with a goal equal to the real cost
   of the first production batch. With a $2,000 budget, the all-or-nothing rule is a safety net:
   you only build what is paid for. Gadget backers (Segment D) are there. State the delivery
   window only when production is booked.
2. **After the campaign: own-site preorders** (the store already built) for late buyers, then
   regular sales for back-to-school 2027. The AI tutor plan always starts on the own site
   (first month free when the calculator is first linked).
3. If you'd rather not crowdfund: **own-site "reserve now, pay when it ships"** (no charge until
   shipping) is the honest low-risk alternative, but it doesn't fund production.

Before any money is taken: refund/return/warranty terms decided (`/refunds/` is still "not
decided"), certification path known, and a lawyer's look at the shell and the terms.

## 13. Compliance checklist (print this)

**Before every post**
- [ ] Only facts from PAGE_BRIEF. No battery life, screen size, ship dates, "in stock",
      reviews, ratings, user counts, awards.
- [ ] No banned words (section 1.4). No cheating jokes. No "hide it" angles.
- [ ] If the SAT/ACT/AP or tests come up: "not allowed on the SAT, ACT or AP; for school tests ask
      your teacher".
- [ ] No AI vendor, payment vendor or Casio name. Casio logo not visible.
- [ ] Renders labelled "CAD render". Screens are real, not mocked up.
- [ ] Price, if shown, includes the plan: $225 + $10/month or $100/year, first month free, no
      card.
- [ ] AI accuracy: "AI can make mistakes" if accuracy comes up. Never "100%".
- [ ] No minors on camera; no faces of minors anywhere.
- [ ] Music is licensed for commercial use.

**Creators and ads**
- [ ] Creator is 18+ (ID or platform verification), and the agreement is signed.
- [ ] Disclosure is in the video itself (spoken and on-screen) and the paid-partnership label is on
      [S46].
- [ ] No payment tied to positive sentiment; no fake reviews, no bought followers or views [S47].
- [ ] Ads target 18+ only. (Under-18 audiences can only get generic age/location targeting anyway
      [S43][S44].)
- [ ] Loaned or free products disclosed.

**Data and sign-ups**
- [ ] Waitlist: email only; a "13 or older" confirmation; no data from under-13s (COPPA [S49]).
- [ ] Collect only what's needed (New York's law limits under-18 data collection [S50]).
- [ ] Every email: postal address, unsubscribe link that works [S55].

**Subscriptions and preorders**
- [ ] Plan terms shown next to the subscribe button: price, renewal, how to cancel. Express
      consent; cancel online; annual reminder for yearly plans (California [S53]; ROSCA [S52]).
- [ ] "First month free" means exactly that: starts at first link, no card needed, the calculator
      says when it's over [S58].
- [ ] Preorders: no ship date without a reasonable basis; delay notices and refunds per the FTC
      Mail Order Rule [S54].

**AI claims**
- [ ] Every claim about the AI can be shown on video today (FTC Operation AI Comply [S48]).
- [ ] No "replaces a tutor", "guaranteed better grades", "never wrong".

**This checklist is not legal advice.** Have a lawyer review the terms, the privacy policy, the
preorder terms and the shell's look before taking money.

## 14. What NOT to do

1. Don't market it for tests, quizzes or exams (except to say it's *not* for the SAT/ACT/AP and to
   explain exam mode).
2. Don't copy VovoCorp's "secret" features, tone or TikTok hashtags (#examenes etc.).
3. Don't buy followers, views, comments or reviews; don't post fake reviews or "customer"
   testimonials before there are customers [S47].
4. Don't use countdown timers, "only X left" or fake price increases.
5. Don't pay or gift product to anyone under 18, and don't run ads with teen interest targeting.
6. Don't mention parents, children or families (owner rule), or aim anything at under-13s.
7. Don't name the AI or payment vendors; don't say "API", "server", "tokens".
8. Don't show the Casio logo or use Casio model names as our brand.
9. Don't show renders as photos, or mock up screens.
10. Don't announce ship dates, battery life or features not yet working (the Humane/Rabbit
    lesson [S31]).
11. Don't DM students, spam Reddit, or post from extra accounts [S56].
12. Don't post daily if it hurts quality; 3 to 5 good posts a week is the sweet spot [S39].
13. Don't spend the budget before the device works on camera.
14. Don't argue with critics; answer with a demo.

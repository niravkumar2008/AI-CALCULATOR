v15 First Power-Up

The power-up guide for the colour-screen board: the v14 board with the e-paper swapped for a 1.9" IPS LCD on socket J5. Do the steps in order, one at a time, the day the boards arrive. Every check is there to catch a fault before it can hurt the board. Ticks are saved in this browser only, separately from the v14 guide.

Which board do I have? The silk in the middle of the component side says AI CALC v15-LCD 2026-10-08, and there is a long 20 mm slot near the right-hand edge with a 30-pin socket J5 next to it. A v14 board says v14 and has J2 for the e-paper: use the v14 First Power-Up Guide for that one. Never flash v15 firmware onto a v14 board, or v14 firmware onto this one.

Five rules for the whole day

  1. If anything gets warm, smells, or a reading is wrong: unplug first, think second.
  2. Never plug or unplug a ribbon (LCD, camera) while the board has any power. Once the battery is in, the board is always powered, so ribbons go in before the battery. general practice
  3. First power comes from a computer USB port, not a phone charger. A computer port cuts the current quickly on a fault; a charger may push 2 A or more.
  4. Never connect the battery until its polarity has been metered (step 4), and never plug the LCD tail in until its finger-1 end has been found with the meter (step 5).
  5. Never burn eFuses, enable secure boot or flash encryption. They are the only things that can lock you out for good.

02Inspect the bare board

Unpowered, under good light (a phone camera zoomed in works). Hold it like the render: component side up, top edge (magnet header) away from you.

What good and wrong look like (loupe checklist)

Use a 10× loupe or the phone camera at 2–3× zoom with the torch on. Compare each part with the drawings, then with zoom A / B / C / D in the board map. JLCPCB also e-mails placement photos: compare those too.

Real renders of these parts are in the board map zooms A–D. A wrongly turned part on a JLCPCB-assembled board is rare, but D7, the ICs and the two sockets are the ones where it would matter.

If a part is turned the wrong way or bridged: don't power it. Photograph it and ask.

03Magnet piece check, before gluing

Unchanged from v14. J3 is wired, from pin 1 (silk N and +, leftmost pin seen from the component side, top edge away from you): VBUS, D−, D+, GND. The last pin has the silk −.

Why: fitted backwards, the cable's 5 V lands on the board's GND. A computer port cuts the current quickly, but a phone charger can push 2 A or more through D7 and the charger chip and damage them. Never test that on purpose.

04Battery polarity

Q1 protects the board from a reversed cell, but with USB also plugged in a few mA would flow into the reversed cell. So: polarity first, always.

05Ribbon checks: camera and LCD tail

Camera ribbon beep test (as v14)

While the camera is out: measure its ribbon from the module's far edge to the tip and note it. Under about 70.3 mm means tape the module up to 1.7 mm towards J1 and drill the 7 mm window where the lens actually sits.

LCD tail: find finger 1 with the diode test

The panel's 30 gold fingers are not numbered on the tail. Plugged in the wrong way round, the screen just stays dark (nothing is damaged, but you lose time). The backlight is four small LEDs between finger 20 (+, the anode) and fingers 21–24 (−, the cathodes). A meter in diode mode lights them a little, and that tells you which end is which. The panel must be off the breakout and unplugged from everything.

Where the dot must end up: J5 is mirrored on purpose (like the camera's J1), so that a tail running naturally through the slot with no twist arrives with finger 1 at the bottom of J5, next to the silk tick (towards Q4), and the LED fingers in the top half (towards Q5). That comes from the panel drawing and has not been seen on a real panel yet confirm on the first panel. If your dot arrives at the top instead, don't plug it in: write it down and ask. The fix is a half twist of the tail (there is about 7 mm to spare) or a footprint swap before the next board order.

07Flash the v15 firmware

The first flash is always over USB, on every board: a new board is blank, and the firmware uses a two-slot partition table (min_spiffs.csv) so that later versions can arrive over the air. An over-the-air update can never write the partition table. Never send a v14 image over the air to a v15 board (or the other way round): the screens are different hardware.

What it looks like

Or type it (VS Code: Terminal > New Terminal, from the repo folder):

cd firmware-v15-lcd %USERPROFILE%\.platformio\penv\Scripts\pio run -t upload %USERPROFILE%\.platformio\penv\Scripts\pio device monitor -b 115200

A good upload ends with Hard resetting via RTS pin... and [SUCCESS]. Then the Serial Monitor; type in the box at the bottom and press Enter:

> status
Firmware: v15lcd-2026.10.10 (slot app0)  id calc-3f9a21      <- right build, first USB flash
Camera: none
Wi-Fi: not set (type wifi, or = on SETUP 6)          <- normal until step 11
AI proxy: not set (type proxy)
Device token: not set (type token)
Battery: 0% (0 mV)                                     <- no cell yet
Screen: 320x170 LCD ok, brightness 60 % (now 60 %), dim after 20 s, ...

Example; the id and some numbers differ on your board.

If no COM port appears, or "no serial data"

In download mode the Serial Monitor shows waiting for download and the COM port number may change. Nothing you do on these pads can damage the board: they are pulled up through resistors. Never touch TP5 (3V3) to TP4.

With a battery connected later, plugging the cable in does not reset the chip, so use the TP7 tap (or unplug the battery first). Last resort: a 3.3 V USB-serial adapter on TP2 (TX) / TP3 (RX) / TP4 (GND), then the same TP1 + TP7 sequence. In deep sleep the USB port disappears: press ON to bring it back.

09LCD, camera, then battery

Unplug the cable before each ribbon. Both sockets are flip-lid and dual-contact: they touch both faces of the ribbon, so it doesn't matter which face of the tail is up. Only which end is finger 1 matters.

LCD into J5 (bench test, panel loose)

On the bench the panel doesn't need to be taped down yet: lay it glass-up on the key side, feed the tail through the slot and into J5 the way the v15 Assembly Guide (step 5) shows. Taping comes later. If you already taped it, that's fine too.

10Backlight current: one reading for the next board

The backlight is fed from 3.3 V through one small resistor, R23 (15 Ω, review 14; was 22 Ω), just above Q5 at the top of J5. How bright it gets depends on the LEDs' own voltage: the ER-TFT019-1 datasheet gives 2.8–3.2 V at 60 mA, which with 15 Ω means about 16–37 mA, 26 mA typical (review 14, item M1). It cannot go above 60 mA, the panel's rating, so nothing can overheat. One reading confirms it; only if it is under about 18 mA and too dim would a later board change R23 (12 Ω if the LED voltage is at least 2.75 V) or add a constant-current driver.

11AI server, and the live preview

The maths works without any of this. For AI SOLVE the calculator needs a phone hotspot (2.4 GHz), the address of your AI server (server/proxy) and its own device token. Putting the server online is exactly as in the v14 guide (its step 10). The calculator never holds the Claude API key.

Live colour preview (new on v15)

Screen messages: Not set up yet: SETUP 6 = address or token missing. NO CONNECTION = the hotspot is off or not 2.4 GHz. AI ACCOUNT = not linked yet, trial over, or the monthly cap. Battery low: plug in the cable to use AI solve = the cell is under 3.6 V: no preview, camera or Wi-Fi until it is back above 3.7 V or a cable is in; maths keeps working.

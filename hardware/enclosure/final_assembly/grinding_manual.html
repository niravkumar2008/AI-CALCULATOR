Shell Grinding Guide

How to grind your Casio fx-115ES shell so the AI-calculator board, camera, battery and magnet connector fit. Three jobs on the back cover (zones 1–3), the magnet notch on the front shell (zone 5), the side-wall jobs on the front shell (zones 6 and 6b), and two "only if your shell needs it" jobs (5b and 6c). Zone 4 (the LR44 cup) is kept, not ground. Every zone below has: why it's needed, the exact numbers, the tool, diagrams, steps with depth-rod checks, how to know you're done, and the usual mistakes. Go slowly: you can check every step with your calipers before you go further.

Read this first

The back-cover floor is only about 1.0 mm thick (your reading D5). You may never go below 0.8 mm. That leaves you just 0.2 mm of room for mistakes when you grind things "flat to the floor". Aim to stop at the floor, never into it.

What you'll need

Cutting

  • Rotary tool with a speed dial (a Dremel or any similar tool). Cordless is fine.
  • Carbide burr, about 3 mm, cylinder or ball shape (1/8″ shank): for taking most of the solar box down and for the magnet notch. Carbide cuts plastic cleanly at low speed; the grey stone bits melt it.
  • Sanding drum, small (about 6 mm), the finest grit you have (120 or finer): for the last few tenths, to get the ground spots flat and smooth.
  • Drill bits: 2 mm pilot, and 7 mm (a 9/32″ bit is 7.1 mm: fine), or a step drill. A 7 mm bit won't fit a rotary-tool collet: use a hand drill or a drill press.
  • Flush cutters (the small electronics side-cutters with one flat face): for the pins and hooks (zone 6b).
  • Needle files: flat and half-round. A flat sanding stick (sandpaper glued on a lolly stick) works too.

Measuring and marking

  • Calipers with a depth rod (the thin rod that slides out of the end). Digital is easiest.
  • A thin steel ruler, to lay across the rim as a reference for depth readings.
  • Fine permanent marker.
  • Masking tape to protect surfaces you are not cutting, and Kapton tape (it doesn't melt).
  • The two printed grind maps at 100 % (see "Templates" below), and the 3D-printed dummy board if you have it.

Holding and safety

  • Safety glasses and a dust mask (FFP2 / N95).
  • A vise or clamp, with a rag or wood between the jaws so the shell isn't scratched or cracked.
  • A spare shell to practise on.

Speed: about 5,000–10,000 RPM

Plastic melts long before it cuts cleanly at high speed. Use the low end of your tool's dial: about 5,000–10,000 RPM. If the dial is printed in thousands (5–35), set it to 5–10. If it is numbered 1–10, start at 1–2 and check your tool's manual for the RPM of each setting. If the plastic smears, strings or goes shiny and soft, you are too fast or pressing too hard: stop, let it cool, slow down. (General rotary-tool advice, not a number from our model; trust what the plastic shows you.)

Templates

  • grind_map_front_shell.svg: lay it in the front shell (keys down), printed side up. Cut it out with its yellow antenna tab.

Jigs (optional, faster and the same every time)

3D-printed jigs in hardware/enclosure/jigs/ (print in PETG if you can; PLA softens where the bit rubs): A back-cover plate (zones 1 + 2, holds A2), A2 camera drill bush (zone 3 pilot), A3 7.2 mm depth gauge (sets the router bit), B magnet-notch saddle (zone 5 + 5b). There is no jig for zones 6, 6b, 6c: they are file and cutter jobs.

Safety

  • Glasses on before the tool is plugged in. Bits shatter and hot chips fly.
  • Mask on. Plastic dust and the smell of hot ABS are not good to breathe.
  • Ventilation: work by an open window or with a fan blowing the dust away from you (not into your face). Vacuum the bench afterwards rather than blowing the dust around the room.
  • Clamp the part. Never hold a small shell in your hand while grinding near your fingers. Soft jaws (rag, wood) and only light clamping pressure: the shell cracks if you squeeze it.
  • Melting plastic = too fast. Smoke, a sharp smell, shiny strings or a blob building up on the bit all mean: stop, let it cool for a minute, turn the speed down, press lighter.
  • Let the tool stop before you put it down. Unplug it when you change bits.
  • Loose sleeves, hair and cords away from the bit.
  • Ground plastic and the bit get hot. Touch them only after they have cooled.
  • No electronics near the shell while you grind. The board goes in only after the shell is washed or blown clean.

How to measure (you will do this a lot)

Almost every step below says depth rod. This is how. You lay a steel ruler across the rim, so you have a flat bridge to measure down from, and you use the calipers' depth rod to measure how far down the plastic is. You never need the absolute number for "flat to the floor": you only compare two readings taken the same way.

The ruler's thickness

If the calipers' end sits on top of the ruler, every reading includes the ruler's thickness t (measure it once with the jaws; usually 0.5–1.0 mm). For "same as the floor" checks (zones 1, 2) that doesn't matter: both readings include it. For a target like "7.0 mm below the rim" (zones 6, 6c), subtract t, or rest the calipers' end directly on the wall's own rim. Measure the same way you did when you got your 4.5–5.0 readings on 2026-10-06, and write down which way that was.

Golden rules

  1. Low speed (5,000–10,000 RPM), light pressure. Let the bit do the work.
  2. Short passes. A few seconds, lift off, look.
  3. Measure every 30 seconds. Blow the dust off, take a depth reading, then decide.
  4. Never grind into the floor. Keep at least 0.8 mm of plastic everywhere (it starts at about 1.0).
  5. Never touch the screw posts, the locating posts (pegs), the snap clips, the outer walls, the window frame or the key holes. Mask them with tape so a slip only scuffs tape.
  6. Plastic melts. If it gets soft or shiny, stop and let it cool for a minute.
  7. Practise on the spare shell first. Do one full zone there before you touch the real one.
Locating posts: hands off

Locating post H6 has only 0.10 mm of room in the board's hole. Don't grind or file any post. If the real board binds on H6, file the hole in the board a little towards the top of the board, never the post.

Zone 1 · back covergrind

Solar box: flat to the floor

Why (what would hit): the box of thin walls (a frame with a grid inside) that hangs down from the back cover sits right over the battery socket J4, the magnet header J3 and the power parts. Without this grind J4 runs 4.6 mm into it and the cover can't close. It is also where the end of the LiPo will lie.

Where

You can't miss it: it's the only box with a grid inside, near the top end of the back cover, beside a corner screw post (top right with the cover inside up and the top end away from you). From the model, its outer frame runs about 6.3 to 19.3 mm down from the back cover's own top outer edge (the outside-face edge, the same datum as the camera hole's 38.0 mm), measured straight down at the middle of the box; because the top edge curves down towards the corners it reads 5.8–18.8 mm at the screw-post end and 6.4–19.4 mm at the far end. Its near end is about 9.9 mm in from the nearest side edge (outside face). Confirm with the back-cover template ("GRIND 1").

Steps (freehand)

  1. Mask the corner screw post and the outer wall next to the box with tape. Clamp the cover inside up, soft jaws, light pressure.
  2. depth rod Reference reading R. Ruler across the rim over the box; rod down to the bare floor next to the box. Write R on the tape.
  3. Burr at 30–45°, 5,000–10,000 RPM. Take the grid walls down first, then the outer frame, a little at a time, working across the whole box so it goes down evenly. Think "lawnmower", not "drill".
  4. depth rod Every 30 seconds: blow off the dust and measure at 4–5 spots over the box. While F is smaller than R, plastic is still standing.
  5. When the walls are about 0.5 mm above the floor (F ≈ R − 0.5), switch to the sanding drum, held flat, and finish gently.
  6. depth rod Final check on a grid: four corners, the middle, and especially the end nearest the screw post (where J4 sits). Every reading = R (± 0.05).
  7. Fingernail test across the old walls: no ridge.

With jig A: drop A in (pins into the 4 boss holes), set a 3.2 mm flat-end router bit with gauge A3 (7.2 mm below the base), run the router inside the window with the shank against the window wall, remove A, then sand the last 0.2 mm skin flat with a sanding block.

Done when

Every reading over the old box equals R (the floor next to it), and you can't feel a ridge with your fingernail. Stop the moment the readings match. Going past R means you are into the floor.

No stub

After this grind, J4 (the battery socket) has only 0.41 mm to the floor. A 0.5 mm stub left behind would hit it. It must be properly flush.

Common mistakes

  • Grinding one corner of the box all the way down first and then gouging the floor next to it. Work the whole area down evenly.
  • Leaving the frame's base "almost flat". It has to be flush.
  • Catching the screw post with the side of the burr. Tape it and keep the burr pointing away from it.
  • Too much speed: the walls melt and fold over instead of cutting. Slow down.
  • Pushing the burr's tip into the floor at the end of a pass: it leaves little pits. Lift off before you reverse.
Zone 2 · back covergrind

Rib B over the camera: 14 mm flat

Why (what would hit): the camera module is 5.4 mm tall and this 1 mm rib runs right across it; without the grind the lens hits the rib by about 0.5 mm and the camera can't sit flat over its window. Only the middle 14 mm of the rib comes off. The rest of rib B stays.

Where

The camera window's centre is on the centre line of the cover's width (measure the full width, halve it), 38.0 mm down from the back cover's own top outer edge, measured along that centre line (from the model; zone 3, "Measuring the drill point", says why older notes said 38.3). Rib B is the middle one of the three long ribs below the solar box; it runs across the cover right there. Mark the centre point on the rib, then mark 7 mm each side of it: that's your 14 mm. The back-cover template shows it as "GRIND 2".

Steps

  1. Before you start: pre-grind check 4 (floor thickness at the moulded label outside, 32–48 mm down from the back cover's own top outer edge). ≥ 0.9 mm: grind flush as below. 0.8–0.9: stop 0.1–0.2 mm proud. Under 0.8: use the spare cover.
  2. Measure the width, halve it, mark the centre on rib B. Mark 7 mm each side. Lay the template in and check the marks agree.
  3. depth rod Reference reading R on the floor next to the rib.
  4. Sanding drum held so it rides along the rib (axis across the rib), 5,000–10,000 RPM, between your marks only. Short passes.
  5. depth rod Every 30 seconds: measure at both ends and the middle of the 14 mm. Stop when F = R everywhere.
  6. Square the two cut ends with a needle file. Fingernail across: no ridge.

With jig A: router inside the Ø16 hole across the rib (it takes 16 mm instead of 14: fine), then scrape the last 0.2 mm off with a flat chisel blade so the camera sits flat.

Done when

The 14 mm stretch reads the same as the floor beside it, at both ends and in the middle. Leave your two marker lines visible: they show you where the camera goes.

Common mistakes

  • Grinding the whole rib instead of just 14 mm.
  • Getting the centre wrong. Measure twice and cross-check with the template before you start.
  • Rounding the floor next to the rib with the edge of the drum. Keep the drum flat and only on the rib.
  • Touching the 4 mm "mid rib" or the rings further down: the camera ribbon passes over them with half a millimetre to spare, but they don't need grinding. Leave them.

Zone 2

Measuring the drill point (which edge, which number)

The hole centre comes from the board: the camera's centre is at KiCad (150, 95.1). The model turns board numbers into shell numbers with X = 150 − x and Y = 138.94 − y (build_final_assembly.py), so the hole is at X = 0 (the width centre line) and Y = 43.84 mm above the shell's middle.

  • Top distance: measure from the navy back cover's own top outer edge, along the centre line: 38.0 mm. In the model that edge is at Y 81.79, and 81.79 − 43.84 = 37.95. This is the number to use with the cover off the calculator, which is how you drill it.
  • Older notes said "about 38.3". That was measured to Y 82.1, the top of the whole calculator's outline, where the silver front shell's top wall stands about 0.3 mm beyond the navy cover's edge. Same hole, different edge. Don't use 38.3 on the loose back cover.
  • Side distance: half of your cover's width, measured at the same height. The model's cover is 78.7 mm across there, so 39.3 mm from each side. Your shell may differ by a few tenths, so measure it and halve it; the two side readings must match (the 7 mm hole has only about 0.7 mm to spare round the lens).
  • Measure to the outer edge, with the caliper square to that edge.
  • Cross-check from the inside: the point must land in the middle of the 14 mm gap in rib B. If the outside number and the gap disagree by more than 0.5 mm, stop and find out why before drilling (both come from the same model point).

Steps

  1. Find the centre on the inside: the middle of your 14 mm marks on the ground rib, on the width centre line. Check it against the template. calipers measure from both side edges: the two numbers must match.
  2. Push a pin or the point of a scriber through to make a small dent at the centre.
  3. Clamp the cover on a scrap block of wood so the drill has something to go into. Put masking tape on the outside where the hole will come out (less chipping).
  4. Drill the 2 mm pilot first, straight up and down (or through the A2 bush in jig A).
  5. Open it to 7 mm with the hand drill, slowly and with light pressure, or from the outside with a step drill (it centres on the pilot). Plastic likes to grab a big bit just as it breaks through: ease off at the end.
  6. Deburr both edges with a half-round needle file or a fingertip of sandpaper. Don't make the hole bigger.
  7. calipers Inside jaws: 7.0–7.1 mm in two directions (round, not oval).
Done when

The hole is round, 7 mm across in both directions (calipers' inside jaws), centred on the marks and clean on both sides.

v15 with the DCXYX camera

The recommended route keeps this hole exactly where it is (the ribbon's extra length goes into an S-fold, see the v15 section). Only if the build uses the fallback "camera moved 8 mm" route does the window move: 8 mm towards the top end (centre about 30.0 mm from the back cover's own top outer edge, still on the centre line). That hole crosses the moulded label's top border outside (cosmetic only: pilot first). Confirm which route before you drill.

Common mistakes

  • Drilling straight in with the 7 mm bit: it wanders and grabs. Always pilot first.
  • Drilling at an angle. Keep the drill upright, or use a drill press.
  • Pushing hard as the bit breaks through: it grabs and cracks the floor round the hole. Ease off.
  • Drilling before the camera route is decided.
  • Later: mounting the camera on foam tape. Use thin tape only (≤ 0.1 mm) under the module, with Kapton on the board below it.
Zone 4 · front shellnot needed (optional)

LR44 coin-cell cup: skip it

Why it's skipped: the final assembly check moved the LiPo battery so it lies flat on the back-cover floor instead of against the front plate. In that position the cup's bottom is still 0.3 mm above the battery, so the cup can stay. Don't grind it.

What the new battery position needs instead

  • The solar-box grind (zone 1): the battery's end sits where the box was.
  • Hold the battery to the floor with thin (0.1 mm) double-sided tape. No foam: the coin-cell cup sits only 0.3 mm above the battery.
  • The battery lies over the old LR44 hole in the back floor. The battery lid still closes that hole.
  • Pre-grind check 3: if the cup is taller than 5.8 mm, the jig-C trim (battery upgrade study) is the fallback.

The front-shell map (regenerated 2026-10-06) now shows the cup in green with "zone 4 (KEEP)". If you still have an older print that says "LR44 cup: TRIM FLAT", throw it away and print the new one.

Zone 5 · front shellcut

Magnet slot: U-notch in the top wall

Why (what would hit): the magnetic charging connector's face (21 × 7 mm) sits flush in the top end wall. Without the notch it runs 1.0 mm into the wall. The notch is 21.5 mm wide, so there is 0.25 mm each side: glue fills that. It is a notch open at the rim, not a closed window: a closed window would leave a strip only 0.45 mm thick at the rim, which would break.

Where

Hold the front shell display side up, top end away from you. On the top end wall, measure from the right-hand side edge: the notch runs from 6.4 mm to 27.9 mm in. That puts it on the same side as the solar window. (Turn the shell face down and look at the wall from outside, like the diagram, and it is still measured from the solar-window corner.) From the rim (the parting line, where the back cover sits) it goes 7.95 mm down towards the display face. Between the notch and the centre are the comb snap tabs: leave them.

Steps (freehand)

  1. Tape the outside of the top wall. Draw the notch on the tape: two vertical lines at 6.4 and 27.9 mm from the solar-window side edge, and the bottom line 7.95 mm from the rim. depth rod against the rim to mark the depth at both ends.
  2. calipers Check the marked width is 21.5 mm before you cut.
  3. Clamp the shell with the top wall facing you, soft jaws, no squeezing on the display window. Tape the corner screw post behind the wall.
  4. With the burr, cut inside your lines, starting at the rim and working down. Stay about 0.5 mm inside the lines.
  5. Finish to the lines with the flat needle file. Keep the bottom edge straight and parallel to the rim.
  6. calipers Width 21.5 at the rim and at the bottom; depth rod depth from the rim 7.95 at both ends.
  7. When the magnet part arrives: its face should sit 0.45–7.45 mm below the rim, flush with the outside. Only glue it after the case closes and the face is flush.

With jig B: clip it over the top wall (shell face down; it wraps the right-hand top corner, so no measuring). Saw down both window edges with a fine razor saw to the window bottom, cut the bottom with flush cutters, file flat to the window's edge with light strokes, round the two inner corners (r 1).

Done when

The notch measures 21.5 mm wide and 7.95 mm deep from the rim, with clean square corners and a straight bottom edge. Too tight is easy to fix; too wide isn't, so creep up on the lines.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring from the wrong edge or with the shell the wrong way up. The notch is on the solar-window side.
  • Cutting to the line straight away with the burr. It always takes a bit more than you think: stay inside, then file.
  • Hitting the top-corner screw post behind the wall. It's close to the notch's outer end: tape it, keep the burr shallow.
  • A sloping bottom edge: the magnet then sits crooked. File it parallel to the rim, checking the depth at both ends.

Why (what would hit): our 3D model guessed a 1.5 mm locating lip round the back cover's rim (it was never measured). If your back cover really has a lip where the magnet sits, the magnet body hits it by about 0.5 mm and the cover won't close over the magnet. Pre-grind check 5 tells you.

Steps

  • Cutting the outer wall of the back cover instead of the lip: the outside would then show a gap.
Zone 6b · front shell · do before 6needed

Snip the 3 round pins and the 3 wire hooks off the inner rib, on BOTH side walls

Why (what would hit): the depth rod (2026-10-06) put the round pins 5.1–5.5 mm (outer two) and 6.0–6.1 mm (middle) below the rim and the U-shaped wire hooks at 5.5 mm; the board occupies 5.5–6.3 mm. So every pin and hook is at board height. On the J2 side their tips are 0–0.5 mm from the board's edge (the paper edge runs through the pin bosses in your photo); on the solar side the two upper hooks sit 1.1–1.4 mm inside the board's top corner and the pins reach 1 mm into the antenna tab. They held Casio's solar-cell wires; nothing of ours runs along those walls (the LiPo leads go straight into J4). Verification 12, hits 1–5 and 8–11.

both screen-section side walls: 3 round pins (25, 34, 44 mm from the shell's own top outer edge at that wall) and 3 hooks (16, 21 and 55–58 mm; the lowest is the wider wire holder)

Close-up of one wall (the other wall has the same set):

Steps

  1. Pull out Casio's old solar-cell wires if they are still in the hooks.
  2. Front shell face down. Find the thin inner rib beside the screen opening on each side; the pins and the U-hooks grow from its inner face.
  3. Flush cutters with the flat face against the rib: snip each pin at its root. Same for the three hooks (cut the arm where it meets the rib; the U falls off). Hold a finger over the piece so it doesn't fly off.
  4. Run a needle file along the rib so nothing stands proud; a fingernail drawn along the rib should not catch.
  5. Repeat on the other wall. Count: 6 pins and 6 hooks in the bin. Blow the chips out.
Done when

Both inner ribs are smooth from the top wall down to where the thick wall ends: 6 pins and 6 hooks gone, no stubs.

Common mistakes

  • Cutters the wrong way round (bevel against the rib): leaves a stub about 0.5 mm long. Flip them.
  • Twisting the cutters: the rib is thin and can crack. Straight squeeze only.
  • Forgetting the J2-side wall. Both walls.
  • Missing the lowest, wider holder (55–58 mm from the top outer edge at that wall).
Zone 6 · front shellneeded

Side-wall relief: 2–2.5 mm off the inner side-wall rib, 14–49 mm from the top (board corner + antenna)

Why (what would hit): on the solar-window side the screen-section side wall is an outer skin, a wire channel and a thin inner rib (the one the three round pins stick out of). Your depth rod (2026-10-06) put the rib's top edge 4.5–5.0 mm below the rim; the board sits 5.5–6.3 mm below the rim, so the rib runs right through where the board goes. Two things on the board reach into it:

  • the board's own top corner, a strip 14–25 mm from the top outer edge that the board keeps at full width: up to 0.7 mm into the rib;
  • the ESP32 module's antenna end, a bare 0.8 mm tab that sticks out 4.15 mm past the board's edge 31–47 mm from the top: 0.3 mm into the rib.

Either one stops the board seating. Only the part of the rib between 4.5 and 7 mm below the rim is in the way, so this is a 2–2.5 mm deep notch along the rib, not the whole rib (verification 12).

Where
solar-window side; 14–49 mm from the shell's own top outer edge, measured along that wall from the rim's outer edge right above the rib (board corner 14–26, antenna tab 30–49). That point is about 1 mm lower than the middle of the top end, because the top end curves down towards the corners.
What
the thin inner rib (1 mm), not the outer skin, not the channel floor
Depth
from the rib's top edge (4.5–5 below the rim) down to 7.0 mm below the rim (6.5 is the minimum: the board's far face is at 6.3)
Pins and hooks
gone first (zone 6b), so the file has a clear run
Tools
flat needle file or a small sanding stick; steel ruler across the rim + depth rod to check

the paper template with its yellow tab lies flat; depth rod on the filed rib reads 7.0 everywhere between the marks

Steps

  1. Do zone 6b first (pins and hooks off, both walls).
  2. Front shell face down, top end away from you. The solar-window side is on your left now (it is the right-hand side when you use the calculator).
  3. Mark 14 and 49 mm down from the top outer edge on the rim of that wall (zero = the rim's outer edge right above the rib, where the top end curves into this wall). Put tape on the outer skin's rim so a slip only scuffs tape.
  4. depth rod Take a "before" reading on the rib's top edge at 20, 30 and 40 mm (should be about 4.5–5.0, measured your usual way).
  5. File the top edge of the inner rib between the marks. Keep the file flat along the rib and push it only lengthwise; the file can't reach the outer skin if it rides on the rib.
  6. depth rod After every few strokes: read at 15, 20, 25 … 50 mm. Aim for 7.0 everywhere (never less than 6.5).
  7. Don't touch the outer skin (the wall you see from outside) or the wire-channel floor.
  8. Lay the paper template in again: the yellow tab and the top corner beside it must drop in flat. Then the dummy board / real board.
Done when

Between 14 and 49 mm from the top outer edge the depth rod reads 7.0 mm on the rib (6.5 minimum), and the yellow paper tab lies flat on the posts with the rest of the template.

Common mistakes

  • Filing the outer skin instead of the inner rib: the outside of the calculator then shows a dip. Look for the thin rib the pins grew from.
  • Stopping at 6.0 because "it looks low enough": the board's far face is at 6.3. Measure.
  • Forgetting the ruler thickness: your 7.0 is then really 6.3. Measure the same way every time.
  • Using the rotary tool here: the rib is 1 mm thin and snaps or melts. Hand file only.
  • Filing outside 14–49 mm: the rest of the rib holds the plate. Leave it.
Zone 6c · front shellonly if needed

In the model the thick wall (and its inner rib) stops at 57.4 mm from the top; your photo of the J2 side suggests it may run to 59–61. If it does, the end of the rib catches the diagonal by up to 0.9 mm. v15-LCD board: what's different

Same zones, same numbers. The v15-LCD board (ER-TFT019-1 panel) fits the ground shell with no new grind, and no grind can be skipped (hardware/enclosure/final_assembly_v15_lcd/REPORT.md). Two things to know:

1. Rib B and the LCD tail's bow

The LCD's flex tail goes down through the slot and makes one U-bow under the board before it goes up into J5. The bottom of that bow passes 0.25 mm over the part of rib B you don't grind (nominal 36.6 mm tail). Each 0.3 mm of extra tail drops the bow 0.15 mm.

  • Measure the tail in assembly step 4. Up to 37.0 mm is fine.
  • If a tail measures over about 37 mm, or the step-10 marker dot shows the bow squeezed by the closed cover: grind zone 2 about 15 mm further towards the slot side, at the same depth (flush with the floor). With the cover inside up and its top end away, the slot side is the left side (the solar box is top right). The bow's lowest point sits about 30 mm left of the centre line: make sure the flat part covers the spot the marker dot shows.
  • Don't fold the tail tighter instead; sharper corners don't fit either.

2. DCXYX camera: keep the S-fold bay clear

If the build uses the DCXYX OV5640-AF camera (longer ribbon), the recommended route keeps the camera and the 7 mm window where they are and tucks the extra ribbon into one S-fold (bend radius 1.2 mm) in the bay between rib C and the small ring on the back cover (KiCad y 108–116, x 147–153: about 51–59 mm from the back cover's own top outer edge, within about 3 mm of the centre line). The ribbon passes only 0.44 mm over the floor there and 0.53 mm under the small ring's top (variant_camera_dcxyx/results.json).

  • Don't grind there (it isn't needed), and don't leave dust, chips, tape, labels or epoxy repairs on that floor.
  • Leave rib C and the small ring alone: they set the bay.
  • If you had to patch the floor near there, sand the patch flush with the depth rod (F = R).

Test fit

1. Paper template

Lay the cut-out grind maps back in each half (cut the front-shell one out with its yellow antenna tab). Every red zone you meant to grind should now sit over bare floor (or the notch), and the antenna tab must lie flat beside the side wall. The green LR44 cup stays.

2. Dummy board (3D print)

hardware/fitcheck/dummy_board.stl is the whole board at real size with every part at its real height, a 12 × 12 × 5.4 mm camera block and a battery block as a separate piece (regenerated 2026-10-06: the loose block is now the 26 × 19.75 × 3.8 mm #1317 at its back-cover-floor spot, and the ESP32 antenna tab is on the dummy because it is part of the module's 3D model). Tallest point: 5.5 mm (J4). Print flat, parts up, 0.2 mm layers, no supports.

  1. Colour the tops of the ribs (and the ground areas) with marker.
  2. Press the dummy in, put the battery piece on the back-cover floor (its new place), close the back cover gently. Don't force it.
  3. Open it. Any marker on the dummy shows a rib that touches. Mark that spot on the shell, take it down a little more, test again.

3. Real board (when it arrives)

  • Fit the board alone first. If it binds on locating post H6, file the board's hole slightly towards the top of the board, not the post.
  • Camera: Kapton on the board, thin tape (≤ 0.1 mm) under the module, centred under the window. Look through the hole: you should see the lens in the middle.
  • LiPo flat on the back-cover floor, held with thin (0.1 mm) tape underneath, nothing on top (only 0.3 mm under the coin-cell cup). Its leads plug into J4 at J4's level, under the board.
  • Camera ribbon: there is little spare (the 3D path needs 58.7 mm from the module's near edge into J1; a nominal 70.5 mm ribbon measured from the module's far edge leaves about 1.4–3.3 mm), so lay it flat and straight into J1. Don't let it sag over the mid rib. If your ribbon measures under about 70.3 mm, the camera moves up to 1.7 mm towards J1 and the window is drilled over the lens (Assembly Guide step 6).
  • Close the case with the screws snug, not tight. It should close fully without a gap at the rim.

Hole through the floorSame, furtherKapton or a thin plastic patch on the outside, epoxy over it from the inside, sand flat. If it's near J4 or the camera, use the spare shell instead.
Cracked wall or ribClamped too hard, twisted the cutters, or the burr grabbedThin super glue (cyanoacrylate) wicked into the crack, or epoxy on the inside; sand flush. A crack through an outer wall or a screw post: use the spare shell.
Plastic melted and smeared, or a blob on the bitToo fast (above ~10,000 RPM) or pressing too hardLet it cool fully, cut the blobs off with a sharp knife, then sand at lower speed. Clean the burr with a brass brush.
Rough, fuzzy or hairy edgesNormal for ABS; worse with a coarse drumScrape with a sharp blade held upright, or a few strokes of a fine needle file. Don't use the rotary tool on fuzz: it melts it.
A ridge or stub you can feelStopped just short, or the drum's edge rode on the floorSanding block held flat, a few strokes. depth rod again.

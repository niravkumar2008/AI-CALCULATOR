Shell Grinding Guide

How to grind your Casio fx-115ES shell so the AI-calculator board, camera, battery and magnet connector fit. Three jobs on the back cover (zones 1–3), one on the front shell (zone 5), plus two "only if your shell needs it" jobs (5b and 6). Zone 4 (the LR44 cup) is kept, not ground. Go slowly: every step can be checked with your calipers before you go further.

Read this first

The back-cover floor is only about 1.0 mm thick (your reading D5). You may never go below 0.8 mm. That leaves you just 0.2 mm of room for mistakes when you grind things "flat to the floor". Aim to stop at the floor, never into it.

What you'll need

Cutting

  • Dremel (or any rotary tool with a speed dial).
  • Carbide burr, cylinder or ball shape: for removing most of the solar box and for the magnet notch.
  • Sanding drum (fine grit if you have a choice): for the last few tenths, to get the ground spots flat and smooth.
  • 7 mm drill bit (a 9/32" bit is 7.1 mm and is fine) plus a small pilot bit. A 7 mm bit will not fit a Dremel collet, so use a hand drill or drill press for it.
  • A flat needle file or sandpaper on a flat stick, if you have one, for squaring the notch corners.

Measuring and marking

  • Calipers with a depth rod (the thin rod that slides out of the end).
  • A steel ruler, to lay across the rim as a reference for depth readings.
  • Fine permanent marker.
  • Masking tape to protect surfaces you are not cutting, and Kapton tape (it doesn't melt).
  • The two printed grind maps at 100 % (see "Templates" below).

Holding and safety

  • Safety glasses and a dust mask.
  • A vise or clamp, with a rag or wood between the jaws so the shell isn't scratched or cracked.
  • A spare shell to practise on.
Speed

Plastic melts long before it cuts cleanly at high speed. Use the low end of your Dremel's dial: about 2–4 on a 1–10 dial. If the plastic smears, strings or goes shiny and soft, you are too fast or pressing too hard: stop, let it cool, slow down. (This is general rotary-tool advice, not a number from our model; trust what the plastic shows you.)

Templates

Two 1:1 maps live in the repo at hardware/fitcheck/:

  • grind_map_back_cover.svg: lay it in the back cover, inside up. It is printed mirrored on purpose.
  • grind_map_front_shell.svg: lay it in the front shell (keys down), printed side up.

Print each from a browser at 100 % / actual size, then measure the 50 mm line on the sheet with your calipers. If it isn't 50 mm, the print is scaled: fix the print settings before you trust it. Cut along the outline and lay it in the shell. The red dashed shapes are what to grind.

Safety

  • Glasses on before the Dremel is plugged in. Bits shatter and chips fly.
  • Dust mask on. Hot plastic dust and fumes are not good to breathe; work by an open window.
  • Clamp the part. Never hold a small shell in your hand while grinding near your fingers.
  • Let the tool stop before you put it down. Unplug it when you change bits.
  • Loose sleeves, hair and cords away from the bit.
  • Ground plastic gets hot. Touch it only after it has cooled.
  • No electronics near the shell while you grind. The board goes in only after the shell is cleaned.

Golden rules

  1. Low to medium speed, light pressure. Let the bit do the work.
  2. Short passes. A few seconds, lift off, look.
  3. Measure every 30 seconds. Blow the dust off, take a depth reading, then decide.
  4. Never grind into the floor. Keep at least 0.8 mm of plastic everywhere (it starts at about 1.0).
  5. Never touch the screw posts, the locating posts (pegs), the snap clips, the outer walls, the window frame or the key holes. Mask them with tape so a slip only scuffs tape.
  6. Plastic melts. If it gets soft or shiny, stop and let it cool for a minute.
  7. Practise on the spare shell first. Do one full zone there before you touch the real one.
Locating posts: hands off

Locating post H6 has only 0.10 mm of room in the board's hole. Don't grind or file any post. If the real board binds on H6, file the hole in the board a little towards the top of the board, never the post.

Zone 1 · back covergrind

Solar box: flat to the floor

Why: the box of thin walls (a frame with a grid inside) that hangs down from the back cover sits right over the battery socket J4, the magnet header J3 and the power parts. Without this grind J4 hits it by 4.6 mm. It is also where the LiPo will lie.

Size
≈ 33 × 12 mm
Height to remove
5–5.5 mm (D6, D13)
Target
Flush with the floor, no stub
Floor left
≥ 0.8 mm (≈ 1.0)
Bits
Carbide burr, then sanding drum
Speed
Low (dial 2–4)

Where

You can't miss it: it's the only box with a grid inside, near the top end of the back cover, beside a corner screw post. From the model, its outer frame runs about 7.2 to 19.2 mm in from the cover's top outer edge, and its near end is about 10.7 mm in from the nearest side edge. Confirm with the back-cover template ("GRIND 1").

Steps

  1. Mask the corner screw post and the outer wall next to the box with tape.
  2. Take a reference reading. Lay the steel ruler across the back cover's rim over the box. Put the end of the calipers on the ruler and slide the depth rod down to the bare floor next to the box. Write this number down: call it R.
  3. With the carbide burr, take the grid walls down first, then the outer frame, a little at a time, working across the whole box so it goes down evenly.
  4. Every 30 seconds: blow off the dust, measure at several spots over the box. While the reading is smaller than R, there is still plastic standing up.
  5. When the walls are about 0.5 mm above the floor (reading ≈ R − 0.5), switch to the sanding drum and finish gently.
  6. Check with a fingertip and the depth rod across the whole area, especially the area nearest the screw post where J4 will sit.
Stop when

Every reading over the old box equals R (the floor next to it), and you can't feel a ridge with your fingernail. Stop the moment the readings match. Going past R means you are into the floor.

No stub

After this grind, J4 (the battery socket) has only 0.41 mm to the floor. A 0.5 mm stub left behind would hit it. It must be properly flush.

Common mistakes

  • Grinding one corner of the box all the way down first and then gouging the floor next to it. Work the whole area down evenly.
  • Leaving the frame's base "almost flat". It has to be flush.
  • Catching the screw post with the side of the burr. Tape it and keep the burr pointing away from it.
  • Too much speed: the walls melt and fold over instead of cutting. Slow down.
Zone 2 · back covergrind

Rib B over the camera: 14 mm flat

Why: the camera module is 5.4 mm tall and this 1 mm rib runs across it; without the grind the lens hits by 0.5 mm. Only the middle 14 mm of the rib comes off. The rest of rib B stays.

Length
14 mm, centred
Height to remove
1 mm
Target
Flush with the floor
Floor left
≥ 0.8 mm (≈ 1.0)
Bits
Sanding drum (burr if slow)
Speed
Low (dial 2–4)

Where

The camera window's centre is on the centre line of the cover's width (measure the full width, halve it), about 38.3 mm down from the cover's top outer edge (from the model). Rib B runs across the cover right there. Mark the centre point on the rib, then mark 7 mm each side of it: that's your 14 mm. The back-cover template shows it as "GRIND 2".

Steps

  1. Mark the 14 mm with two marker lines. Put tape on the floor on both sides of the rib if you like.
  2. Reference reading R next to the rib, exactly as in zone 1.
  3. Sand the rib down between your marks with the sanding drum, held so it rides along the rib.
  4. Check every 30 seconds until the reading on the old rib equals R.
Stop when

The 14 mm stretch reads the same as the floor beside it. Leave your two marker lines visible: they show you where the camera goes.

Common mistakes

  • Grinding the whole rib instead of just 14 mm.
  • Getting the centre wrong. Measure twice and cross-check with the template before you start.
  • Touching the 4 mm "mid rib" further down: the camera ribbon passes over it with half a millimetre to spare, but it does not need grinding. Leave it.
Zone 3 · back coverdrill

Camera window: drill Ø 7 mm

Why: the camera looks out of the back. Nothing hits here; this hole is the camera's view. The lens does not need to poke into the hole.

Tools

Speed
Slow
Do after
Zone 2

Steps

  1. Find the centre on the inside: the middle of your 14 mm marks on the ground rib, on the width centre line. Check it against the template.
  2. Push a pin or the point of a scriber through to make a small dent at the centre.
  3. Clamp the cover on a scrap block of wood so the drill has something to go into. Put masking tape on the outside where the hole will come out (less chipping).
  4. Drill a small pilot hole first, straight up and down.
  5. Open it to 7 mm with the hand drill, slowly and with light pressure. Plastic likes to grab a big bit just as it breaks through: ease off at the end.
  6. Clean the edge with a fingertip of sandpaper. Don't make the hole bigger.
Stop when

The hole is round, 7 mm across (check with the calipers' inside jaws) and clean on both sides.

Common mistakes

  • Drilling straight in with the 7 mm bit: it wanders and grabs. Always pilot first.
  • Drilling at an angle. Keep the drill upright, or use a drill press.
  • Later: mounting the camera on foam tape. Use thin tape only (≤ 0.1 mm) under the module, with Kapton on the board below it.
Zone 4 · front shellnot needed (optional)

LR44 coin-cell cup: skip it

Why it's skipped: the final assembly check moved the LiPo battery so it lies flat on the back-cover floor instead of against the front plate. In that position the cup's bottom is still 0.3 mm above the battery, so the cup can stay. Don't grind it.

What the new battery position needs instead

  • The solar-box grind (zone 1): the battery's end sits where the box was.
  • Hold the battery to the floor with thin (0.1 mm) double-sided tape. No foam: the coin-cell cup sits only 0.3 mm above the battery.
  • The battery lies over the old LR44 hole in the back floor. The battery lid still closes that hole.

The front-shell map (regenerated 2026-10-06) now shows the cup in green with "zone 4 (KEEP)". If you still have an older print that says "LR44 cup: TRIM FLAT", throw it away and print the new one.

Zone 5 · front shellcut

Magnet slot: U-notch in the top wall

Why: the magnetic charging connector's face (21 × 7 mm) sits flush in the top end wall. Without the notch it runs 1.0 mm into the wall. The notch is 21.5 mm wide, so there is 0.25 mm each side: glue fills that.

Width
21.5 mm
Depth from rim
7.95 mm
Through
the 1.2 mm wall
From right edge
6.4 → 27.9 mm
Bits
Carbide burr, then file
Speed
Low (dial 2–4)

Where

Hold the front shell display side up, top end away from you. On the top end wall, measure from the right-hand side edge: the notch runs from 6.4 mm to 27.9 mm in. That puts it on the same side as the solar window. From the rim (the parting line, where the back cover sits) it goes 7.95 mm down towards the display face. It's a notch open at the rim, not a closed window: a closed window would leave a strip only 0.45 mm thick at the rim, which would break.

Steps

  1. Tape the outside of the top wall. Draw the notch on the tape: two vertical lines at 6.4 and 27.9 mm from the right edge, and the bottom line 7.95 mm from the rim. Use the calipers' depth rod against the rim to mark the depth at both ends.
  2. Check the marked width is 21.5 mm before you cut.
  3. Clamp the shell with the top wall facing you, soft jaws, no squeezing on the display window.
  4. With the burr, cut inside your lines, starting at the rim and working down. Stay about 0.5 mm inside the lines.
  5. Finish to the lines with the file or sanding drum. Keep the bottom edge straight and parallel to the rim.
  6. Measure: width 21.5, depth from the rim 7.95 at both ends.
  7. When the magnet part arrives: its face should sit 0.45–7.45 mm below the rim, flush with the outside. Only glue it after the case closes and the face is flush.
Stop when

The notch measures 21.5 mm wide and 7.95 mm deep from the rim, with clean square corners. Too tight is easy to fix; too wide isn't, so creep up on the lines.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring from the left edge or with the shell upside down. Display up, top away, measure from the right.
  • Cutting to the line straight away with the burr. It always takes a bit more than you think: stay inside, then file.
  • Hitting the top-corner screw post behind the wall. It's close to the notch's outer end: tape it, keep the burr shallow.
Zone 5b · back coveronly if there

Back-cover lip under the magnet notch

Why: our 3D model guessed a 1.5 mm locating lip round the back cover's rim (it was never measured). If your back cover really has a lip where the magnet sits, the magnet body hits it by 0.5 mm.

Steps

  1. Look at your back cover's rim at the top end. No raised lip there? You're done with this zone.
  2. If there is one: put the back cover on the notched front shell and mark the lip with the marker through the notch, on both sides.
  3. Take the lip off between the marks, 21.5 mm wide, down to the back cover's rim. Burr, then file. Don't touch the outer wall.
Stop when

The marked stretch of lip is level with the rim and 21.5 mm wide, lined up with the notch.

Zone 6 · front shellneeded

Side-wall relief: 2–2.5 mm off the inner side-wall rib, 15–50 mm from the top (board corner + antenna)

Why: on the solar-window side the screen-section side wall is an outer skin, a wire channel and a thin inner rib (the one the three round pins stick out of). Your depth rod (2026-10-06) put the rib's top edge 4.5–5.0 mm below the rim; the board sits 5.5–6.3 mm below the rim, so the rib runs right through where the board goes. Two things on the board reach into it: the board's own top corner, a strip 15–26 mm from the top outer edge that the board keeps at full width (up to 0.7 mm into the rib), and the ESP32 module's antenna end, a bare 0.8 mm tab that sticks out 4.15 mm past the board's edge 32–48 mm from the top (0.3 mm into the rib). Either one stops the board seating. Only the part of the rib between 4.5 and 7 mm below the rim is in the way, so this is a 2–2.5 mm deep notch along the rib, not the whole rib (verification 12).

solar-window side; 15–50 mm from the top outer edge (board corner 15–27, antenna tab 31–50)
What
the thin inner rib (1 mm), not the outer skin, not the channel floor
Depth
from the rib's top edge (4.5–5 below the rim) down to 7.0 mm below the rim (6.5 is the minimum: the board's far face is at 6.3)

flat needle file or a small sanding stick; steel ruler across the rim + depth rod to check

Steps

  1. Do zone 6b first (pins and hooks off, both walls).
  2. Front shell face down, top end away from you. The antenna side is on your left now (it is the right-hand, solar-window side when you use the calculator).
  3. Mark 15 and 50 mm down from the top outer edge on the rim of that wall.
  4. Between the marks, file the top edge of the inner rib down until the depth rod reads 7.0 mm from the ruler on the rim. Keep the file flat along the rib; check every 10 mm.
  5. Don't touch the outer skin (the wall you see from outside) or the wire-channel floor.
  6. Lay the paper template in again: the tab and the top corner beside it must drop in flat. Then the dummy board / real board.
Stop when

Between 15 and 50 mm from the top the depth rod reads 7.0 mm on the rib, and the yellow paper tab lies flat on the posts with the rest of the template.

Zone 6b · front shellneeded

Snip the 3 round pins and the 3 wire hooks off the inner rib, on BOTH side walls

Why: the depth rod (2026-10-06) put the round pins 5.1–5.5 mm (outer two) and 6.0–6.1 mm (middle) below the rim and the U-shaped wire hooks at 5.5 mm; the board occupies 5.5–6.3 mm. So every pin and hook is at board height. On the J2 side their tips are 0–0.5 mm from the board's edge (the paper edge runs through the pin bosses in your photo); on the solar side the two upper hooks sit 1.1–1.4 mm inside the board's top corner and the pins reach 1 mm into the antenna tab. They held Casio's solar-cell wires; nothing of ours runs along those walls (the LiPo leads go straight into J4). Verification 12, hits 1–5 and 8–11.

Where
both screen-section side walls: 3 round pins (26, 35, 45 mm from the top) and 3 hooks (17, 22 and 56–59 mm from the top; the lowest one is the wider wire holder)
What
cut each pin and hook off flush with the rib it grows from
Leave
the rib itself (zone 6 only notches it on the solar side), the posts, the snap tabs
Tools
flush cutters; a needle file for the stubs
Time
2 minutes

Steps

  1. Front shell face down. Find the thin inner rib beside the screen opening on each side; the pins and the U-hooks grow from its inner face.
  2. Flush cutters flat against the rib: snip each pin at its root. Same for the three hooks (cut the arm where it meets the rib; the U falls off).
  3. Run a needle file along the rib so nothing stands proud; a fingernail drawn along the rib should not catch.
  4. Repeat on the other wall. Blow the chips out.
Stop when

Both inner ribs are smooth from the top wall down to where the thick wall ends: 6 pins and 6 hooks gone.

Zone 6c · front shellonly if needed

Lower end of the inner ribs, where the board widens

Why: the board goes from 64.75 mm wide (beside the screen) to its full width between 59.5 and 63 mm from the top, with a diagonal on each side. In the model the thick wall (and its inner rib) stops at 58.4 mm from the top; your photo of the J2 side suggests it may run to 60–62. If it does, the end of the rib catches the diagonal (up to 0.9 mm). The paper dry fit tells you: today the paper's wide corner on the J2 side did not lie flat beside the lowest hook; zone 6b removes that hook, and this zone is for what is left.

Test fit

1. Paper template

Lay the cut-out grind maps back in each half (cut the front-shell one out with its yellow antenna tab). Every red zone you meant to grind should now sit over bare floor (or the notch), and the antenna tab must lie flat beside the side wall. The green LR44 cup stays.

2. Dummy board (3D print)

hardware/fitcheck/dummy_board.stl is the whole board at real size with every part at its real height, a 12 × 12 × 5.4 mm camera block and a battery block as a separate piece (regenerated 2026-10-06: the loose block is now the 26 × 19.75 × 3.8 mm #1317 at its back-cover-floor spot, and the ESP32 antenna tab is on the dummy because it is part of the module's 3D model). Tallest point: 5.5 mm (J4). Print flat, parts up, 0.2 mm layers, no supports.

  1. Colour the tops of the ribs (and the ground areas) with marker.
  2. Press the dummy in, put the battery piece on the back-cover floor (its new place), close the back cover gently. Don't force it.
  3. Open it. Any marker on the dummy shows a rib that touches. Mark that spot on the shell, take it down a little more, test again.

3. Real board (when it arrives)

  • Fit the board alone first. If it binds on locating post H6, file the board's hole slightly towards the top of the board, not the post.
  • Camera: Kapton on the board, thin tape (≤ 0.1 mm) under the module, centred under the window. Look through the hole: you should see the lens in the middle.
  • LiPo flat on the back-cover floor, held with thin (0.1 mm) tape underneath, nothing on top (only 0.3 mm under the coin-cell cup). Its leads plug into J4 at J4's level, under the board.
  • Camera ribbon: there is little spare (the 3D path needs 58.7 mm from the module's near edge into J1; a nominal 70.5 mm ribbon measured from the module's far edge leaves about 1.4–3.3 mm), so lay it flat and straight into J1. Don't let it sag over the mid rib. If your ribbon measures under about 70.3 mm, the camera moves up to 1.7 mm towards J1 and the window is drilled over the lens (Assembly Guide step 6).
  • Close the case with the screws snug, not tight. It should close fully without a gap at the rim.

If you went too far

What happenedFix
Thin spot or small dip in the floor (still solid)Fill with 2-part epoxy or plastic filler from the inside, let it cure fully, sand flat to the floor. Re-check with the depth rod: a bump here can hit J4.
Hole through the floorKapton or a thin plastic patch on the outside, epoxy over it from the inside, sand flat. If it's near J4 or the camera, use the spare shell instead.
Magnet notch too wideThe glue fills small gaps. If it's more than about a millimetre, build the side up with epoxy and file it back.
Notch too deepFine for fitting; fill the gap under the magnet with glue when you glue it in.
Camera hole off-centre or too bigMove the camera module to sit under the hole (it's held by tape). If it's badly off, use the spare back cover.
Nicked a post, clip or the outer wallStop. A small nick can be epoxied. A cracked or broken screw post: use the spare shell.
Plastic melted and smearedLet it cool fully, cut the blobs off with a sharp knife, then sand at lower speed.
Remember

You have a spare shell. A mistake costs you a shell, not the project.

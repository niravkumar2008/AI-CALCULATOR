Calculator Assembly Guide

How to turn a tested v14 board, a ground fx-115ES shell and the bought parts into a finished calculator. Build one calculator at a time, in order. Tick each box as you go. Ticks are saved in this browser only, separately for calculator 1 and calculator 2.

Every picture opens full size when you tap it. Labels: suggestion = my idea, the project docs say nothing. unsure = not checked on real parts, or the docs disagree. Times are rough.

5 rules for the whole build
  1. No power while you plug a ribbon. Never plug or unplug a ribbon (e-paper or camera) while the board has any power. Once the battery is in J4, the board is always powered. So both ribbons go in before the battery.
  2. Glue the magnet piece last. Only after its meter check has passed.
  3. Never grind, file or force a post. If the board sticks on post H6, file the board's hole a little towards the top of the board.
  4. Keep TP1, TP4 and TP7 clear. No tape, foam or glue on these three test pads. They are your only way to rescue a board that won't take firmware.
  5. Never burn eFuses, and never turn on secure boot or flash encryption.

··Words you'll meet

Ribbon (FPC)
The thin, flat, flexible cable on the e-paper panel and on the camera. FPC means "flexible printed circuit". It has tiny gold fingers at its tip. Bend it gently; never crease it sharp.
Connector latch (lid)
The small hinged bar on a ribbon socket (J1, J2). Lift it to open the socket, slide the ribbon in, press it flat to lock. A ribbon never goes in or out while the lid is closed.
J1, J2, J3, J4
Names printed on the board for its connectors. J1 = camera ribbon. J2 = e-paper ribbon. J3 = magnetic charging piece. J4 = battery plug.
Test pad (TP)
A small round bare copper spot on the board, labelled TP1, TP2… You touch a meter probe or a wire to it. TP1 = BOOT, TP4 = GND (ground, 0 V), TP7 = EN (reset).
Polarity
Which wire is + and which is −. Battery and charging power only work one way round. Backwards can damage parts.
VBUS / GND
VBUS is the +5 V wire from a USB cable. GND is ground, the 0 V wire.
Kapton
Thin, orange, heat-proof tape that doesn't conduct electricity. You put it between parts so nothing bare touches.
Meter "beep" mode
The continuity setting on your multimeter. It beeps when the two probes are connected by metal.
Key side / component side
The board's two faces. Key side: the flat side with the key pads, facing the keys and the screen. Component side: the side with all the parts, facing the back cover.

00Overview: what goes where

The new board sits in the front shell exactly where Casio's board was. Its key side faces the rubber key mat. The e-paper screen is taped to the key side, behind the display window. Its ribbon goes through a slot in the board to J2 on the other side. The camera, the battery and the magnetic charging piece are all on the component side, facing the back cover.

01Take the Casio apart

≈ 20 min
You'll needSmall Phillips screwdriver, a soft cloth, a cup for screws, side cutters, a marker, a clean bag for the key mat.

You keep the outside and throw out the insides. Keep: front shell, back cover, rubber key mat, key caps, display window, battery lid, rubber feet, all 6 screws. Remove: the Casio board, the LCD screen module, the LR44 coin cell. The docs don't describe how this Casio comes apart, so these steps are general practice suggestion.

only the empty shell halves, the key mat, the key caps, the small parts and 6 screws are left, labelled 1 or 2.

02Grind the shell

≈ 1–2 h + drying
You'll needThe grinding kit, safety glasses and a mask (all listed in the grinding guide), calipers.

Before grinding: the 10-minute pre-grind checklist

hardware/verification/07_final_review_fitment.md lists the few shell dimensions the 3D model only guessed. Measure them on your shell before the first cut: the depth of the inner side-wall rib (decides zone 6), the flat strip between the solar window and the display window (must be at least 2.6 mm: the e-paper panel's top edge lies there), the floor thickness at the moulded label on the back (the camera hole and rib B are inside it), whether the back cover has a lip at the magnet notch (zone 5b), and the screw length. Write the numbers on the template.

Camera hole: drill now or later?

Measure the camera ribbon from the module's far edge to its tip (bring-up step 5). 70.3 mm or more: drill at the normal spot (centre of rib B) now. Shorter: wait. In step 6 the camera moves up to 1.7 mm towards J1, and you drill where the lens really ends up.

all planned zones are ground, nothing else is touched, and the shell is clean and dry.

03Test the bare board first

see the power-up guide
You'll needThe First Power-Up Guide, the board, the e-paper panel, the camera, the battery, the magnet piece and cable, the multimeter, the PC.

Before anything goes into a shell, the bare board must pass the First Power-Up Guide: inspection, meter checks, first power on USB, flashing the firmware, the self-test, then screen, camera and battery one at a time. This guide doesn't repeat it.

If your e-paper panel is still on the Waveshare HAT

The HAT is the blue test board from the prototype. The bare panel's ribbon is held in the HAT's socket by a latch. Free it before the power-up guide connects it to J2.

Must pass before assembly
  • Power good: TP5 reads 3.25–3.35 V, TP7 high, nothing warm.
  • Firmware flashed; status works in the Serial Monitor.
  • Self-test with screen and camera connected: display_ok, camera_ok, keypad_scanner_ok, wifi_ok all true. The 50-key test waits until the key mat is on (step 8).
  • Battery charging seen in status ("(charging)").
  • AI settings in (power-up guide step 10): status shows your hotspot, the proxy address and a device token; account shows the calculator linked.

the board passed every check above and is now unpowered.

05E-paper screen onto the board

≈ 20 min
You'll needE-paper panel, double-sided tape about 0.15 mm thick, scissors, a fingernail or plastic pick, the front shell (for a dry fit).

The panel lies on the board's key side, screen facing out through the window. Its ribbon leaves the panel's short end, folds back under the panel, runs about 6.6 mm to a 1.0 × 14 mm slot, goes through the board and turns straight into J2 on the component side.

The ribbon is short

It is 14.3 mm long (Waveshare drawing, ±0.3). The 3D model's path from the glass edge into J2 needs 12.7 mm with the tip 2 mm inside J2, so the nominal spare is about 1.6 mm; with a 3 mm insertion and a short ribbon it is nearer 0.3 mm. Settled (review 07): treat it as no spare: the fold hugs the panel end, the ribbon hugs the slot's outer wall, and it goes fully into J2. If you measured the stiff end longer than 5 mm before ordering, follow what Claude told you then.

Window mask: hides the ribbon and glass edges

Through the clear window you would see the ribbon's root at the left end and the glass edges. A thin matte-black sticker on the inside of the window lens hides everything except the picture. It sits 1.79 mm above the screen, so it never touches it.

You'll needThe template window_mask_template.svg (also in the repo at hardware/enclosure/final_assembly/), matte black sticker paper or black vinyl (0.08–0.15 mm), a steel ruler, a new craft-knife blade, isopropyl alcohol.

the panel is taped flat, centred in the window on the dry fit, its ribbon is locked in J2 with no sharp crease, and from the front you see only the picture inside the black mask.

06Camera onto the board

≈ 15 min (+ 20 min if you drill now)
You'll needCamera, thin double-sided tape (0.1 mm or less, not foam tape), tweezers, the back cover; calipers and drill if the hole waits for this step.

The camera sits on the component side, lens pointing at the back cover, centred under the 7 mm hole (on the case's centre line). Its ribbon lies flat and runs straight to J1.

If the camera fails laterJ1's pin order is about 90 % sure. If camera_ok is false after reseating once, the known fix is a 180° twist in the ribbon. The power-up test should already have settled this.

the camera is stuck down under the hole, the lens shows in the middle of the hole, and the ribbon is flat and locked in J1.

07Magnet piece into J3

≈ 10 min
You'll needThe magnet piece (VBUS leg marked), side cutters, the multimeter on beep.

J3 is a sideways socket at the board's top edge, its holes facing the top wall. The magnet piece's legs push into it. Later its face is glued flush in the U-notch. The glue takes the cable's pull, never the legs.

Why polarity matters hereBackwards, the cable's 5 V lands on the board's GND. A phone charger can then push 2 A or more through D7 and the charger chip. Never test that on purpose.

the marked leg is in pin 1, the pin-4 leg beeps to TP4, and the piece sits square on the board edge.

09Battery

≈ 10 min
You'll needThe Kapton-wrapped battery, the 0.1 mm tape strip, the back cover for a test fit.

The battery (Adafruit #1317, 150 mAh) lies flat on the back-cover floor, in the top-left corner as seen from the front, beside the board. Its long side (26 mm) runs left to right and the wire end faces right, towards the middle of the cover and J4. The wires stay below the board's level and the plug goes straight into J4. From now on the board is always powered.

Finding the spot: push the battery's top edge up against the lip of the top wall, then slide it left until there is a 2.5 mm gap (a 2.5 mm drill shank is a good feeler) between its left edge and the round screw post in the corner. The first rib across the cover should then show as a 1 mm sliver below the battery. The battery bridges over the old battery-lid opening; that's fine.

the plug is home in J4, the battery is snug under the test-fitted cover, and TP1/TP4/TP7 are bare.

Reopening safely

Taking the board out againThe docs don't cover this. The magnet is glued in the shell and its legs sit sideways in J3, so lifting the board straight up would bend the legs. Soften or cut the glue first (hot glue is easier), lift the board and magnet out together, then pull the legs out of J3 suggestion.

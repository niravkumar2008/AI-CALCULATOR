v15 Assembly Guide

How to turn a tested v15-LCD board, a ground fx-115ES shell and the bought parts into a finished calculator with the colour screen. Same order as the v14 (e-paper) guide; the screen, camera-choice and checking steps are new or explained in more depth. Build one calculator at a time, in order. Tick each box as you go. Ticks are saved in this browser only, separately for calculator 1 and calculator 2, and separately from the v14 guide.

Every step has the same shape: time and you'll need, then numbered sub-steps (each with a picture, a why note and its tick boxes), a green check before moving on box, an amber common mistakes box, and a done when line. Every picture opens full size when you tap it. Labels: suggestion = my idea, the project docs say nothing. unsure = not checked on real parts, or the docs disagree. Times are rough. No v15 calculator has been built yet: the 3D pictures come from the Fusion model of the final v15-LCD board; drawings marked "diagram" are simple sketches, not to scale.

6 rules for the whole build
  1. No power while you plug a ribbon. Never plug or unplug a ribbon (LCD or camera) while the board has any power. Once the battery is in J4, the board is always powered. So both ribbons go in before the battery.
  2. Never crease the LCD tail. It goes down the slot and makes one soft bow to J5. No folds, no Z-fold, no pinching. A crease can break the tracks inside it, and you can't see that until the screen fails.
  3. Finger 1 to the silk tick. The LCD tail goes into J5 with its finger-1 end (found with the meter) at the bottom of J5, next to the small silk tick. Never guess.
  4. Glue the magnet piece last. Only after its meter check has passed. Never "turn it over" to make it fit.
  5. Never grind, file or force a post. If the board sticks on post H6, file the board's hole a little towards the top of the board. And keep TP1, TP4 and TP7 clear of tape, foam and glue.
  6. Never burn eFuses, and never turn on secure boot or flash encryption.

··Words you'll meet

Each word has a small picture; the same parts appear big in the steps below.

LCD (panel)
The new colour screen: a 1.9-inch, 320 × 170 pixel panel (49.72 × 25.80 mm, 1.43 mm thick). It has its own light behind the picture (the backlight).
Tail (FPC)
The thin, flat, flexible cable that comes out of the LCD, 15.5 mm wide and 36.6 mm long, with 30 gold fingers at its tip. FPC means "flexible printed circuit". Bend it gently over a fingertip; never crease it.
Stiffener
The last 4.5 mm of the tail is backed with a stiff strip, so the tip can be pushed into the socket. That part stays straight. Your finger-1 dot goes on it.
Finger 1
The gold finger at one end of the tail. It must go to pad 1 of J5. You find it with the meter's diode test (step 4) and mark it with a dot.
Connector latch (lid)
The small hinged bar on a ribbon socket (J1, J5). Lift it to open the socket, slide the ribbon in, press it flat to lock. A ribbon never goes in or out while the lid is closed.
J1, J3, J4, J5
Names printed on the board for its connectors. J1 = camera ribbon. J3 = magnetic charging piece. J4 = battery plug. J5 = LCD tail (new on v15; v14's J2 is gone).
Slot
The long narrow hole near the board's edge (1 × 20 mm). The LCD tail goes through it from the key side to the component side.
Bow
The one soft U-shaped curve the tail makes under the board. All the spare tail length (about 6.4 mm) lives in it. Its lowest point hangs about 4.7 mm below the board.
Rib B
A low plastic wall inside the back cover. Its middle 14 mm is ground flat over the camera (grinding zone 2). The un-ground part beside it sits just 0.25 mm under the tail's bow: the tightest spot in the build.
Kapton
Thin, orange, heat-proof tape that doesn't conduct electricity. You put it between parts so nothing bare touches.
ESD
Static electricity. A spark you don't even feel can kill the LCD's driver chip. Touch something grounded (a radiator, the PC case) before you handle the panel, and don't work on carpet in socks or a fleece.
Test pad (TP)
A small round bare copper spot on the board, labelled TP1, TP2… TP1 = BOOT, TP4 = GND (ground, 0 V), TP7 = EN (reset).
Polarity
Which wire is + and which is −. Battery and charging power only work one way round. Backwards can damage parts.
Meter modes
"Beep" (continuity) beeps when two points are connected by metal. "Diode" mode (symbol →|) pushes a tiny current and shows the voltage it needs: a white LED shows about 2.4–2.9 V.
Key side / component side
The board's two faces. Key side: the flat side with the key pads, facing the keys and the screen. The LCD is taped here. Component side: the side with all the parts, facing the back cover.
Position jig
A paper template (lcd_position_jig_v15.svg) that you lay on the key side: its window shows exactly where the panel goes, its cut-out shows the slot.
Window mask
A thin matte-black sticker on the inside of the clear window lens. Its 43.72 × 23.70 mm opening shows only the picture and hides the panel's edges and tail.
Pill
The black carbon dot under each rubber key dome. When you press a key, the pill bridges the gold fingers of the key pad on the board.
S-fold, tuck bay
Only for the DCXYX camera: its ribbon is about 8 mm longer, so the spare length is folded into a soft S that sits in the "tuck bay", a floor pocket in the back cover between rib C and the small round ring.
Protection board
A tiny circuit board on the battery's wire end, usually under the tape. It cuts the battery off if it is shorted, over-charged or run flat. Never cut it off.

00Overview: what goes where

The board sits in the front shell exactly where Casio's board was, key side towards the rubber key mat. The LCD is taped to the key side, behind the display window. Its tail goes down through a slot in the board, makes one soft bow under the board and plugs into J5 on the other side. The camera, the battery and the magnetic charging piece are all on the component side, facing the back cover, exactly as on v14.

01Take the Casio apart

≈ 20 min
You'll needSmall Phillips screwdriver, a soft cloth, a cup for screws, side cutters, calipers, a marker, a clean bag for the key mat, a tray for loose key caps.

Identical to v14. You keep the outside and throw out the insides. Keep: front shell, back cover, rubber key mat, key caps, display window, battery lid, rubber feet, all 6 screws. Remove: the Casio board, the Casio LCD module, the LR44 coin cell. The docs don't describe how this Casio comes apart, so these steps are general practice suggestion.

1.1Battery out, screws out≈ 5 min

a loose coin cell can short across the Casio board while you cut wires; and the plastic clips break if you pry instead of finding the last screw.

1.2Casio board and LCD out≈ 10 min

the black dots (pills) under the domes are what close each key on the new board. Skin grease on them makes keys unreliable.

1.3Measure and label≈ 5 min

Check before moving on
  • Coin cell out, no Casio electronics left in either half.
  • All 6 screws in the cup; screw length written down.
  • Key mat clean and bagged; caps all present.
Common mistakes
  • Prying the back cover instead of finding the 6th screw: snapped clips.
  • Pulling the key mat by a dome: tears it.
  • Mixing the halves of calculator 1 and 2: the ground parts no longer match.

only the empty shell halves, the key mat, the key caps, the small parts and 6 screws are left, labelled 1 or 2.

02Grind the shell

≈ 1–2 h + drying
You'll needThe grinding kit, safety glasses and a mask (all listed in the grinding guide), calipers, flush cutters, and your camera choice (Seeed or DCXYX, see step 6), because it decides where the camera hole goes.

The grinding is exactly the same as for v14. The 3D check of the v15 model found nothing of the LCD, its tail or J5 touching the un-ground shell, so there is no new grind and none can be skipped. Follow the Shell Grinding Guide step by step: it has the tools, safety rules, before/after pictures and the depth to check for each zone. In short:

2.1Back cover: zones 1, 2 (and 5b)≈ 45 min

rib B carries on beside the ground part, and the LCD tail's bow hangs over that un-ground part with only about 0.25 mm to spare. Grinding it now "just in case" weakens the cover for nothing; step 4 (tail length) and step 10 (marker check) tell you if it is needed.

2.2Camera hole (zone 3): where, and when≈ 20 min

Decide the camera first
  • Seeed, ribbon 70.3 mm or more (far edge → tip): drill at the normal spot now.
  • Seeed, shorter: wait. In step 6 the camera moves up to 1.7 mm towards J1, and you drill where the lens really ends up.
  • DCXYX with the S-fold (recommended): the camera stays at the normal spot: drill there now.
  • DCXYX fallback (camera moved 8 mm): the hole is drilled 8 mm further from J1 (towards the top of the calculator). Only choose this if the S-fold has been tried and won't sit. Don't drill the normal hole first.

a drilled hole can't be moved. The whole point of the camera choice is where the lens sits, so the drill waits until you know.

2.3Front shell: zones 5, 6b (6, 6c only if needed)≈ 40 min

Rib B: the one thing to leave alone for now

The un-ground part of rib B (seen from the inside, to the right of the camera hole) sits under the LCD tail's bow. In the model the bow clears its top by about 0.25 mm with a 36.6 mm tail, about 0.1 mm with a 36.9 mm tail, and touches from about 37.1 mm. Don't grind more of it now. Only if step 4 measures a tail over 37.0 mm, or step 10's marker check shows the bow squeezed, grind zone 2 extended: the same 1 mm depth, about 15 mm further towards that side (the slot side) rib height from the replica model.

Check before moving on
  • Floor ≥ 0.8 mm everywhere you ground.
  • No stub left on the solar box (zone 1): J4 has only 0.41 mm.
  • Camera hole drilled where your camera choice needs it, or deliberately not yet.
  • Both halves clean and dry.
Common mistakes
  • Grinding all of rib B "to be safe": not needed, and it can't be put back.
  • Drilling the camera hole before choosing the camera (DCXYX fallback needs it 8 mm away).
  • Leaving dust in the key holes: dead keys in step 8.
  • Filing a post because the board is tight: never. File the board hole (step 8).

all planned zones are ground, nothing else is touched, and the shell is clean and dry.

03Test the bare board

≈ 15 min here + the power-up guide
You'll needThe v15 First Power-Up Guide, the board, the LCD (bare), the camera, the battery, the magnet piece and cable, the multimeter, the PC, a plastic card.

Before anything goes into a shell, the bare board must pass the v15 First Power-Up Guide: inspection, meter checks, first power on USB, flashing firmware-v15-lcd, the self-test, then screen, camera and battery one at a time. This guide doesn't repeat it. The ER-TFT019-1 (and the Sungood and Goldenmorning panels) arrive bare, so there is nothing to free: skip sub-step 3.2.

3.1Know the board before you test it≈ 5 min

every later step names these parts. Two minutes with these pictures saves plugging the wrong socket.

3.2Only if your panel is on an Adafruit #5394 breakout: taking it off≈ 10 min

On the breakout, the bare panel is held on the front by double-sided tape, and its tail wraps round the board's edge into a 30-pin socket on the back. You want only the panel. The socket type is read from Adafruit's PCB files, not seen yet: check when yours arrives

Must pass before assembly
  • Power good: TP5 reads 3.25–3.35 V, TP7 high, nothing warm.
  • firmware-v15-lcd flashed; status shows v15lcd-….
  • LCD tail diode test done, finger-1 corner marked on the stiffener (power-up step 5 = this guide's step 4.5).
  • Self-test with LCD and camera connected: colour bars seen, backlight sweep seen, lcd_ok, backlight_ok, camera_ok, keypad_scanner_ok, wifi_ok all true. The 50-key test waits until the key mat is on (step 8).
  • Panel flags settled (colours right way round), written on the bag.
  • Battery charging seen in status; AI settings in (power-up step 11).
Check before moving on
  • Every line in the red box is true for this board.
  • The board has no power: no battery, no cable.
Common mistakes
  • Skipping the power-up guide because "it's the same board": a fault found inside a closed case costs an hour.
  • Unplugging a ribbon with the battery still in J4.

the board passed every check above, the panel is bare (or free of its breakout) with finger 1 marked, and the board is unpowered.

04Prepare the parts

≈ 45 min
You'll needMultimeter (with diode mode), two sewing pins, phone charger, the #5412 cable, flat pliers, a fine marker, calipers, Kapton tape, scissors, thin (0.1 mm) double-sided tape, a printer. Touch something grounded before the LCD.

Most of these checks are in the power-up guide (its steps 3–5). If you did them there, just tick them here. Do them on the real parts of each calculator. The LCD part is explained in more depth here because it decides the rest of the build.

4.1Magnet piece: find VBUS with the meter≈ 10 min

the piece looks the same both ways round, and its legs fit J3 both ways. Only the meter knows which leg carries 5 V. Backwards, 5 V lands on the board's ground.

4.2Battery: polarity and wrap≈ 10 min

generic cells are often wired the other way round in the plug. Reversed, the battery fights the charger. If reversed: swap the two crimp pins in the plug (lift each tab with a needle, slide out, swap, click back), then meter again. No soldering.

4.3Camera: beep test, ribbon length, which camera≈ 10 min

the ribbon length decides the camera position and route, and the camera position decides the hole in the back cover. One measurement now avoids a wrong hole.

4.4LCD: handling the bare panel≈ 3 min

  • ESD first: touch a radiator or the PC case before you pick it up, and again if you stood up and walked. No carpet, no fleece, no plastic tray rubbed on clothes. Keep the panel in its anti-static bag until you need it suggestion.
  • Hold it by the two short edges, glass and backlight together, between finger and thumb. Never by the tail, never by the glass alone (the backlight can come away).
  • No fingers on the gold fingers of the tail: skin grease makes a poor contact in J5.
  • Protective film: the clear film on the glass protects the polarizer from scratches and fingerprints. Leave it on through steps 4–7. Peel it in step 8, just before the board goes into the shell: peel slowly from the tab (fast peeling makes static), then blow off dust and don't touch the glass again.
  • Fingerprint on the glass after peeling? Wipe once, lightly, with a dry microfibre cloth; only if that fails, a cloth barely damp with isopropyl, no pressure suggestion.
  • Store it glass side down on a soft cloth, tail lying flat. Don't bend the tail at its root.

the driver chip is right at the tail root and is the part static kills; the tail root is where a bend cracks the tracks. Both failures are invisible until the screen stays dark.

4.5LCD: find finger 1 with the diode test (fingers 20 and 22)≈ 10 min

The 30 gold fingers are not numbered. Plugged in the wrong way round, the screen just stays dark (nothing is damaged, but you lose time). The backlight is small white LEDs between finger 20 (+, anode) and finger 22 (−, cathode on the ER-TFT019-1; 21, 23, 24 are unused). A meter in diode mode lights them a little, and that tells you which end is which.

  1. Touch something grounded. Panel glass down on a soft cloth, gold fingers up.
  2. Meter on diode mode (→|): red lead in VΩ→|, black in COM. A sewing pin pushed onto each probe tip.
  3. Pick one end of the fingers and call it end A. Red probe on the 11th finger from end A, black on the 9th. Light pressure, no scratching.
  4. Reading of about 2.4–2.9 V: end A is the 30 end. "OL": try from the other end. Neither end reads: swap the probes once. Still nothing: stop and ask (wrong panel type?).
  5. Fine marker: a small dot on the stiffener (the stiff back of the tip) at the finger-1 corner. Never on the gold.

the dot is the only thing that tells you, in step 5, which way round the tail goes into J5. The meter pushes about 1 mA: enough for a faint glow, never enough to harm the LEDs. 2.4–2.9 V is a typical white LED, not from the datasheet

4.6LCD: measure the tail length≈ 3 min

Tail over 37.0 mm: zone 2 extended grind

Grind rib B in the back cover about 15 mm further from the zone-2 gap towards the slot side (seen from inside: to the right of the camera hole), at the same depth (flush with the floor, floor still ≥ 0.8 mm), with the same tools as zone 2 in the grinding guide. Never shorten or fold the tail to make room, and 2 mm-radius bends are not an option either (they would hang even lower).

the bow hangs right over the un-ground part of rib B. A longer tail makes a deeper bow; squeezed by the closed cover, it can crack.

4.7LCD: tape strips, jig and mask template≈ 10 min

a printer set to "fit to page" shrinks the jig by a few percent, and then the panel ends up off-centre in the window. The 50 mm bar catches that.

Check before moving on
  • Magnet VBUS leg marked; battery polarity positive on the red wire.
  • Camera ribbon length written down, and you know Seeed or DCXYX.
  • Finger-1 dot on the LCD stiffener; diode reading written down.
  • Tail length written down and ≤ 37.0 mm (or the zone-2 extended grind is planned).
  • Jig and mask printed at 100 % (50 mm bar measured).
Common mistakes
  • Measuring the tail from the frame or backlight edge instead of the glass edge (adds about 0.6 mm on some panels).
  • Counting fingers from the wrong end and still marking: if neither end gives 2.4–2.9 V, stop.
  • Dot on the gold: the ink can spoil the contact. Dot on the stiffener only.
  • Swapping magnet legs when the meter shows −5 V: swap the probes instead.
  • Foam tape anywhere: it is 1 mm thick; the gaps are 0.1–0.5 mm.

the magnet leg is marked and straight, the battery is checked and wrapped, the camera ribbon is measured, the LCD's finger 1 is marked, the tail is measured, and the tape, jig and mask template are ready.

05LCD onto the board, tail into J5

≈ 45 min
You'll needThe LCD (finger 1 marked, film still on), the two 0.1 mm tape strips, the paper jig, a fingernail or plastic pick, tweezers, a soft cloth, the front shell (for a dry fit), the mask template and black sticker material. Touch something grounded first.

The panel lies flat on the board's key side, screen facing out through the window. Its tail leaves the panel's short end, runs about 5 mm on the key side to a 1.0 × 20 mm slot, goes straight down through the board, makes one soft bow under the board (towards the back cover) and comes back up into J5, the stiff tip going straight in. The tail is 36.6 mm; the direct route is about 30.2 mm, so the bow takes up about 6.4 mm. In the model it hangs about 4.7 mm below the board, with bends of about 1.2 mm radius: a soft U, not a fold.

5.1Clean the key side and lay the jig≈ 5 min

the panel's picture must sit centred in the 43.72 × 23.70 mm mask opening; the jig puts it there to within a fraction of a millimetre without measuring.

5.2Tape the panel and place it≈ 10 min

pressing only lightly first lets you correct the position after the dry fit. The glass is thin: pressure in the middle can crack it or mark the picture.

5.3Feed the tail down the slot: ONE gentle bow≈ 5 min

the copper tracks in the tail survive a soft curve many times, but a crease can crack them at once, and you can't see the crack: the screen fails later, maybe only when the case is pressed.

5.4J5: open the latch, check the dot≈ 3 min

J5 is mirrored on purpose so that a tail running naturally through the slot, with no twist, arrives the right way round. If it doesn't, something differs from the drawing, and a half twist (there are about 7 mm to spare) is a decision to make together, not a guess.

5.5Insert about 2 mm, square; close the latch≈ 3 min

a tip that goes in at a slant touches the wrong contacts on one side; a tip only half in loses contact when the case is pressed. Square and fully in, the latch clamps all 30 fingers evenly.

5.6Check the bow (and know the rib-B spot)≈ 2 min

If the tail is longer or shorter than 36.6 mm

Each extra mm of tail makes the bow about 0.5 mm deeper. The model has about 0.25 mm between the bow and the un-ground part of rib B with a 36.6 mm tail; at 36.9 mm about 0.1 mm, and from about 37.1 mm the bow touches the rib. Touching lightly is harmless; being squeezed by the closed cover is not. You check it in step 10. If it squeezes: zone 2 extended grind (step 4.6), never a fold.

5.7Quick screen test (optional here, required in step 8)≈ 3 min

Only if the magnet piece is still pushed into J3 from the power-up guide: snap the cable on (no battery) and type selftest, q to skip the keys. Colour bars, grey ramp and border appear, then the backlight sweeps up. Cable off again before going on. Otherwise this test happens in step 8.

5.8The v15 window mask≈ 15 min

Through the clear window you would see the panel's black glass border, the tail end and the board around the panel. A thin matte-black sticker on the inside of the window lens hides everything except the picture. It sits about 1.5 mm above the panel, so it never touches it. The v15 opening is centred, not off-centre like v14's.

You'll needThe template window_mask_template_v15.svg (in the repo at hardware/enclosure/final_assembly_v15_lcd/), matte black sticker paper or black vinyl (0.08–0.15 mm), a steel ruler, a new craft-knife blade, isopropyl alcohol.

cutting the opening first, while the sheet is still big and flat, keeps its corners square. The opening is 0.5 mm bigger than the picture all round, so a slightly off-centre panel still shows its whole picture.

Viewing angleThe whole picture shows straight on and up to about 19° off to the side; tilt it further and the mask edge starts to cover the far edge of the picture (a few rows at 30°). That's normal for a mask above the glass if it bothers you: cut the opening at 45.5 × 24.0 for about ±43°, the panel's own black border then shows.

Check before moving on
  • Panel taped flat, centred in the window at the dry fit, film still on.
  • Tail: down the slot, one soft bow ≤ about 5 mm deep, no kink at the slot.
  • Finger-1 dot at J5's silk tick, tip fully in, latch flat, tug passes.
  • Mask on the lens, even frame, TOP towards the solar cell.
Common mistakes
  • Bending the tail at the panel edge instead of at the slot.
  • Pulling the tail to the middle of the slot: its 0.5 mm offset is normal.
  • Pushing the tail into J5 with the latch closed: it won't go in, and it bends the fingers.
  • Taping the bow down or pressing it flat "to make room".
  • Pressing the middle of the glass when sticking the panel.
  • Using the v14 mask: wrong opening position.

the panel is taped flat and centred in the window, its tail goes down the slot in one soft bow into J5 with finger 1 at the silk tick, the lid is closed, and the mask is on the lens.

06Camera onto the board

≈ 15 min Seeed · ≈ 35 min DCXYX (+ 20 min if you drill now)
You'll needCamera, thin double-sided tape (0.1 mm or less, not foam tape), Kapton, tweezers, a plastic pick, the back cover; for the DCXYX also a 2.4 mm drill bit (its smooth shank is the bending rod) and a ruler; calipers and drill if the hole waits for this step.

The camera sits on the component side, lens pointing at the back cover, centred under the 7 mm hole. Its ribbon runs to J1 near the bottom of the board. The LCD tail's bow is beside it, about 4 mm away: they don't touch. Which route the ribbon takes depends on the camera.

6.1Stick the camera down (both cameras)≈ 5 min

6.2Seeed: straight route into J1≈ 5 min

the back cover's middle rib passes only 0.48 mm under a flat Seeed ribbon. A loop of slack there gets pinched when the cover closes.

6.3DCXYX: pre-bend the S-fold and place it in the tuck bay≈ 20 min

The DCXYX ribbon is about 8 mm longer than the Seeed's (about 11 mm more than the straight path needs). Instead of a loose loop, that spare length is folded into one tidy S that sits in the tuck bay: the floor pocket in the back cover between rib C (camera side) and the small round ring (J1 side), at KiCad y 108–116, x 147–153. On the real board that is straight down the ribbon's line from the camera: the fold starts about 9 mm past the camera module's edge (13 mm from the camera centre) and ends about 21 mm from the camera centre.

  1. Ribbon beep test done (step 4.3). Camera stuck at the normal spot (6.1).
  2. Lay the ribbon flat towards J1 and mark lightly on its back (not the gold) where the fold starts: about 9 mm past the camera module's edge marking: suggestion.
  3. Hold the 2.4 mm drill bit by its shank across the ribbon at the mark. Bend the ribbon slowly round the shank, about 180°: fold 1. Slide the bit out.
  4. A few millimetres on, bend the other way round the shank: fold 2. You now have a soft S. Never pinch a bend flat with fingers or pliers.
  5. Check the free end now reaches J1 straight and flat with the S in place, then plug it into J1 exactly as in 6.2 (latch up, square, all the way, latch down, light tug).
  6. Put a strip of Kapton on the floor of the tuck bay in the back cover, under where the S will sit. Hold the straight part of the ribbon flat to the board with a small strip of Kapton just past the fold, so the S stays over the bay second strip: suggestion.
  7. Lay the back cover on (no screws) and look in from the side: the S sits in the bay, nothing pinched against the ring wall or rib C.

a loose loop of 11 mm would land wherever it likes, maybe across the middle rib (0.48 mm) or the LCD bow. Pre-bending on a rod gives a known, gentle radius (1.2 mm, the same as the LCD bow corners) and puts the spare length in the one pocket that has 6 mm of free height and no board parts.

6.4DCXYX fallback: move the camera hole 8 mm≈ 25 min, only if needed

Only if the S-fold won't sit (it springs open, or the cover crushes it): the camera and its 7 mm window move 8 mm away from J1 (towards the top of the calculator, KiCad y 87.1). The ribbon then runs straight with about 3.5 mm slack, like the Seeed. Shell drill only: no board change.

  • The camera sits on bare board there (nearest parts: Q5 1.55 mm, J5 2.45 mm, R22 3.7 mm): Kapton under it as usual.
  • The lens then ends up about 1.9 mm from rib A on the back cover: don't move further than 8 mm (limit about 8.9 mm).
  • Dress the battery leads tight at the plug exit in step 9, or they touch the camera's focus motor.
  • Outside, the moulded label's border crosses the new hole: pilot-drill first; it is only cosmetic.
  • If you already drilled the normal hole: cover it from inside with black tape suggestion, and ask first.

it is the one known-good fallback from the model: it needs no new board and no fold, only a hole in a different place.

6.5Drill (if waiting) and look through the hole≈ 5 min (+ 20 drilling)

If the camera fails laterJ1's pin order is about 90 % sure. If camera_ok is false after reseating once, the known fix is a 180° twist in the ribbon. The power-up test should already have settled this.

Check before moving on
  • Camera on thin tape, centred under the hole (within 0.8 mm).
  • Ribbon square in J1, latch flat, tug passes.
  • Seeed: flat, no loop. DCXYX: one S in the tuck bay on Kapton, not crushed with the cover laid on.
  • Camera ribbon at least about 4 mm from the LCD bow.
Common mistakes
  • Foam tape under the camera: the lens hits the cover.
  • DCXYX ribbon left as a loose loop, or folded flat with a crease instead of bent round the shank.
  • Fold in the wrong place (over the middle rib instead of the tuck bay).
  • Twisting the ribbon to "make it reach" J1.
  • Drilling the fallback hole before trying the S-fold.

the camera is stuck down under the hole, the lens shows in the middle of the hole, and the ribbon is locked in J1: flat (Seeed) or with one S-fold in the tuck bay (DCXYX).

07Magnet piece into J3

≈ 10 min
You'll needThe magnet piece (VBUS leg marked, meter check passed in step 4.1), side cutters, the multimeter on beep.

Unchanged from v14. J3 is a sideways socket at the board's top edge, its holes facing the top wall. The magnet piece's legs push into it. Later its face is glued flush in the U-notch. The glue takes the cable's pull, never the legs.

7.1Line up: N end = pin 1 = VBUS≈ 3 min

"Never turn it over": if the marked leg doesn't line up with pin 1 when the face points at the notch, something is wrong with the mark or the piece, not with the direction. Turning the piece over to make it fit is exactly how 5 V ends up on ground. Re-do the meter check instead.

7.2Push in and beep≈ 5 min

the beep test proves, on the board itself, that ground went where ground belongs, before any power is ever applied through this piece.

Why polarity matters hereBackwards, the cable's 5 V lands on the board's GND. A phone charger can then push 2 A or more through D7 and the charger chip. Never test that on purpose.

Check before moving on
  • Marked leg in pin 1 (N / +).
  • Pin-4 leg beeps to TP4; pin-1 leg does not.
  • Body square to the board edge; not glued yet.
Common mistakes
  • Turning the piece over so the legs "line up better".
  • Gluing now: glue waits for the key and screen test in step 8.
  • Bending the legs sideways when pushing: straighten first (step 4.1).

the marked leg is in pin 1, the pin-4 leg beeps to TP4, and the piece sits square on the board edge.

08Key mat and board into the front shell

≈ 40 min + glue setting time
You'll needSoft cloth, the front shell with key caps (mask on the lens), the key mat, the board with LCD, camera and magnet on it, the back cover, 2 screws, the magnetic cable and PC, epoxy or hot glue, a blower or soft brush.

8.1Key mat in≈ 5 min

8.2The moved SHIFT, ALPHA and ON keys≈ 2 min (look only)

On v14 these three pads sat a little off the Casio pills (85–100 % on the pad). On the final v15 board they were moved so each pill is 100 % on its pad and still about 90–97 % on even if the mat sits 0.7 mm off. Nothing to do here except keep the mat flat and clean.

if one of these three keys is missing in the test below, the cause is dust or a lifted mat, not the pad position. (In the 3D pictures the replica mat's domes still sit on the old pad centres: that only affects the pictures.)

8.3Peel the LCD film, board in≈ 10 min

the film comes off at the last possible moment so the glass collects no dust or fingerprints before it is sealed behind the lens. Peeling slowly limits the static charge the film makes.

8.4Magnet face flush≈ 3 min

8.5Screen and key test before gluing (cable power, no battery)≈ 10 min

once the magnet is glued, getting the board out again is slow. Test everything you can while it is still free.

8.6Glue the magnet≈ 5 min + setting

Check before moving on
  • Board flat on every post; H6 not forced.
  • Self-test: full pattern inside the mask, backlight sweep, 50 keys, keys_missing [].
  • Magnet face flush, glued, glue set.
Common mistakes
  • Forcing the board past H6 or filing the post.
  • Peeling the film early and then handling the glass.
  • Gluing before the key test.
  • Glue running into J3 or onto the face.

09Battery

≈ 15 min
You'll needThe Kapton-wrapped battery (polarity checked in step 4.2), the two 0.1 mm tape strips, a 10 × 15 mm Kapton strip, a 2.5 mm drill shank as a feeler, the meter on DC volts, the back cover for a test fit.

The battery lies flat on the back-cover floor, in the top-left corner as seen from the front, beside the board. Its long side runs left to right and the wire end faces right, towards J4. From now on the board is always powered. (The LCD tail's bow is more than 5 mm from the battery.)

9.3Place and tape the battery≈ 8 min

Alternative cell: TW302030A Taiwoo TW302030 (140 mAh, 30 × 20 × 3.0 mm) also fits, in the CAD at both its nominal and worst-case size (32 × 20.5 × 3.3). Three conditions: place it 1 mm further left than the #1317 (left edge about 1.7 mm from the corner post, top edge against the lip); if it measures over about 20.3 mm wide, push its top edge right up to the lip so it clears rib A; and its leads must leave the short (lead) end. The same tape L and lead route work. It must have a protection board and a JST-PH plug wired pin 1 = GND, pin 2 = +; meter it before plugging in.

Finding the spot: push the battery's top edge up against the lip of the top wall, then slide it left until there is a 2.5 mm gap (a 2.5 mm drill shank is a good feeler) between its left edge and the round screw post in the corner.

  • Battery flat on the floor on 0.1 mm tape, not squeezed by the cover.

Common mistakes

  • Foam pad under or on the battery.
  • Leads looped over J4 or the camera ribbon: the cover presses them.

  • Probes touching each other while metering a cell.

the plug is home in J4, the battery is snug under the test-fitted cover, and TP1/TP4/TP7 are bare.

Taking the board out againThe docs don't cover this. The magnet is glued in the shell and its legs sit sideways in J3, so lifting the board straight up would bend the legs.

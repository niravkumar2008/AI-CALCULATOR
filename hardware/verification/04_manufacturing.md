# 04 — Manufacturing verification (JLCPCB PCB + PCBA), stage 13b

> **⚠ Superseded by stage 14: see `../FINAL_STATUS.md` and `05_stage14_recheck.md`.** M1/M2 (CPL rotations) are fixed: `fab/` CPL is generated by `tools/jlc_cpl.py`. In JLCPCB's preview rotate nothing; only verify.

Date: 2026-10-04. Board `kicad/ai_calc.kicad_pcb` (saved 04:09), fab files `fab/` (generated 04:11).
Method: gerbers unzipped and parsed with gerbonara, rasterised (20 px/mm) and checked with exact vector geometry (shapely); board data read with KiCad 10 `pcbnew` Python; DRC re-run with kicad-cli; JLCPCB footprints fetched with `easyeda2kicad` for every LCSC number and fitted against the KiCad footprints; live stock and parameters from JLCPCB's parts API. Nothing in `kicad/` or `fab/` was changed.

## Verdict

**Do not upload the CPL in `fab/` as-is.** 8 of the 77 placements will come out wrong in JLCPCB's frame:
- 7 rotated ICs and transistors, including the charger (U2), the 3.3 V regulator (U3) and both camera LDOs.
- J3, whose body would face into the board.

Use `verification/ai_calc_CPL_JLCPCB_corrected.csv` instead, and still check the preview.

The gerbers, drills, BOM and LCSC numbers are good to order. One copper-to-hole clearance on the key side is below JLCPCB's rule, but it is cosmetic: expect a question from JLCPCB, not a dead board.

With the corrected CPL and a preview check, the order is **GO**.

## Findings, by severity

### FATAL (if not corrected)

**M1. CPL rotations for the SOT packages: 7 parts would be placed with pin 1 in the wrong corner.**
- JLCPCB places each part using the EasyEDA footprint of its LCSC number. Those footprints have a different zero orientation from KiCad's.
- The corrections were derived from each part's EasyEDA pad coordinates, matched pin by pin to the KiCad footprint. Each one gives an exact match (residual ≤ 0.2 mm, which is only pad-size differences).
- **Not all SOT-23-5 parts need the same correction:**
  - The "-BL" EasyEDA footprints (U2, U3, U7) are drawn with pin 1 bottom-left and the pins in horizontal rows, so they need **+270°**.
  - The "-BR" footprints (U4, U5) and the SOT-23 (Q1, Q2) need **+180°**.
  - The previous review's "probably 180° for all SOT parts" is wrong for U2, U3 and U7. A 180° click on those would still be 90° off.
- **What happens if they aren't corrected:**
  - U3 (RT9080) reversed puts SYS into VOUT, so there is no 3.3 V.
  - U2 reversed means the charger is dead.
  - Q1 reversed means no battery path.
  - U7 is on the USB lines.

**M2. J3 (magnet socket, C46061768) needs 180°.**
- In the EasyEDA footprint the socket body sits on the **+y side of the pads** (courtyard 2.55–11.05 mm below the pad row). In the KiCad footprint the body runs **towards the top edge** (−y).
- The pads are a symmetric row of 4, so JLCPCB would happily place it at 0°. The body would then face into the board, over D1/D6/R20/U7, with the openings pointing inward.
- At 180° the body points over the top edge as designed. The socket isn't polarised (4 identical contacts), so the reversed "pin numbers" don't matter electrically: contact positions = pads.
- Confidence medium-high: it's based on EasyEDA's silk and courtyard, not the 3D model.

### LIKELY PROBLEM

**M3. Key pad SW1 (B.Cu) is 0.055 mm from the 6.0 mm NPTH hole H3.**
- Where: the bottom bar of SW1 pad 2 (COL0), at x 173.6–179.6, y 122.445–123.045. H3 is at (174.0, 126.1), radius 3.0. See `renders/zoom_SW1_H3_npth_clearance_bottom.png`.
- JLCPCB wants ≥ 0.2 mm from NPTH to copper.
- KiCad DRC did **not** flag it (0/0/0, see below), so the hole-clearance rule apparently isn't applied between this NPTH pad and this SMD pad. It's a real geometry fact in the gerber.
- **Effect:** JLCPCB's CAM will either pull the copper back about 0.2 mm locally or email an engineering question (which holds the order). Electrically harmless: a sliver of one finger of a key contact.
- **Recommendation:** answer "OK to clip copper locally" if asked. Optionally fix in KiCad first by moving H3 or trimming that bar; that is a design change, so not done here.
- SW50 vs H1 is 0.255 mm and the zones are 0.25 mm. Those pass.

**M4. Edge rails / conveyor.**
- U1 overhangs the left edge by about 4.2 mm: the module spans x ≈ 114.7–135.2, and the board edge there is at x ≈ 118.9.
- The left edge also carries C32, D7 and C7 within about 4 mm of it.
- JLCPCB needs clear conveyor edges, so it will very likely add rails (paid) or ask.
- A breakaway rail on the **left** edge would sit under the antenna overhang and is awkward to snap off. Ask for rails only on the short (top/bottom) edges, or accept "no rails" if they agree.
- J3 also overhangs the top edge (see C3), so the top rail must leave J3 free. In practice, **rails on the right edge + bottom edge** is the safe answer.

### CHECK

- **C1. Silkscreen pin-1 marks.**
  - The pin-1 marks on J1, J2, J4, L1 and Q3 use 0.06 mm silk lines (JLCPCB minimum about 0.15 mm), so they may not print.
  - Most other silk is 0.12 mm, which normally prints.
  - It's only cosmetic, but it's the reference JLCPCB's engineer compares against. Use `fab/render_top.png` / the assembly PDF when checking the preview.
- **C2. 0.8 mm board, 71.7 × 148.9 mm, with an LGA module.**
  - Thin, long boards warp in reflow. That's the main risk for U1's hidden pads.
  - Standard PCBA includes X-ray for BGA/QFN-type parts. Ask for **X-ray of U1 and U6** in the order notes (U6 = QFN EP).
  - Keep 0.8 mm only if the enclosure requires it.
- **C3. J3 body position.**
  - The EasyEDA drawing puts the body 1.0 mm further from the pads than the KiCad model does (2.55 vs 1.55 mm).
  - The part will land on the pads either way. If EasyEDA is right, the body overhangs the top edge by about 1.0 mm instead of 0.4 mm. Check with the magnet dry fit.
- **C4. J1 reversed numbering.**
  - J1 uses the `_CamReversed` footprint, so its pad numbers are mirrored relative to the C6364666 EasyEDA footprint.
  - **Geometry** matches exactly at the CPL's 180° (the mounting tabs fix it; at 0° it would be 3.2 mm off).
  - JLCPCB's preview will show EasyEDA "pin 1" at KiCad pin 24's end. That's correct: **do not let them rotate J1**. Tell them the numbering is intentionally reversed and they should align by the pads and mounting tabs.
- **C5. Solder-mask expansion is 0 everywhere.**
  - Every pad does have its own opening (checked pad by pad: 329 top pads, 556 key pads, 0 covered).
  - The dams between 0.5 mm-pitch pads are 0.2 mm, which is within JLCPCB's green-mask capability.
- **C6. TVS D7 leakage.**
  - SMF5.0A has Vrwm 5 V and Ir up to 400 µA (JLCPCB data). With 5.25–5.5 V adapters it conducts a little more, but only while plugged in.
  - Harmless for this design.

### OK (verified)
